## The objection at full strength

Some vegans say sentience is the line. But insects have brains, and are killed in huge numbers in farming; oysters and mussels have no central brain at all. If you are consistent, either insects matter or oysters are fine. If you can't decide, doesn't that show the whole project is arbitrary?

## Response

This is a fair challenge, and the honest answer is different for each animal.

Insects first. A [2022 review](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/117533) applied eight criteria for sentience to insect orders. Adult flies, mosquitoes, cockroaches and termites met six of the eight, which the authors call strong evidence for pain. Adults of most other orders met three to four, which they call substantial evidence, and they found no good evidence that any insect failed a criterion, with big gaps especially for juveniles. The [New York Declaration](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration) also lists insects among animals with a realistic possibility of consciousness. So insects are not a trivial case, and vegans can accept that.

Bivalves are different. A 2022 [Wageningen report](https://edepot.wur.nl/577988) found no papers directly showing whether bivalves are sentient or not, although a few studies on predator avoidance, learning and serotonin suggest they might be. That is uncertainty, not evidence either way. Some vegans eat bivalves for that reason; others do not.

Neither case leaves the argument arbitrary. Moral concern follows the evidence of sentience. It is strong for the pigs, chickens and cows that make up most animal agriculture, substantial for many insects, and open for oysters. Not being able to settle the last case tells us nothing about the first.

## In short

- Insect sentience evidence is substantial, though less than for mammals and birds.
- For bivalves, direct evidence is absent either way.
- Uncertainty at the edge does not touch the clear cases.
