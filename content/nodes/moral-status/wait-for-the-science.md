## The objection at full strength

Researchers still disagree about where sentience begins. Until they agree, demanding that people give up animal products is premature, and the sensible policy is to wait.

## Response

Scientific agreement about borderline cases has always lagged behind. If we waited for it to be complete we would never act, and in practice governments have not waited. The UK reviewed the evidence and extended legal protection to cephalopods and decapods: the [LSE review](https://www.lse.ac.uk/News/News-Assets/PDFs/2021/Sentience-in-Cephalopod-Molluscs-and-Decapod-Crustaceans-Final-Report-November-2021.pdf) recommended that all of them be regarded as sentient for the purposes of UK animal welfare law.

More importantly, the open questions are at the edges. The [New York Declaration](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration) says there is strong scientific support for conscious experience in mammals and birds. Whatever is eventually decided about insects, that does not change.

So waiting makes sense for the hard cases, as a reason for further study and caution. It does not make sense as a reason to postpone changes about the ones where the science is already clear.

## In short

- Disagreement at the edge doesn't touch the core cases.
- Governments acted on the evidence before it was complete.
- Strong support for mammals and birds already exists.
