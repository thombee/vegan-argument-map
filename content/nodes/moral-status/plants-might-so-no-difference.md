## The objection at full strength

You cannot prove plants are not sentient, so there is no certain difference between eating a carrot and eating a calf. If suffering is the issue, every diet involves killing something that might feel.

## Response

That is right about proof, and the sensible response is to weigh probabilities. The evidence that animals like pigs and hens feel is strong; the case that plants do is contested by plant biologists, as [reported here](https://www.sci.news/biology/plant-consciousness-07359.html).

Then add the arithmetic. According to [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets), 41% of the world's cereals go to animal feed, only 7% of soy goes to human foods, and beef converts about 2% of feed energy into beef. A diet built on animals runs through a much larger quantity of plants than a diet of plants. If there is any chance plants matter, the plant-based diet is the one that involves fewer.

So you do not need to resolve the debate. On any assumption about plants, from "they feel nothing" to "they might feel something", the case against farming animals stands.

## In short

- A small chance for plants versus a strong case for animals.
- Feeding plants to animals uses far more plants than eating them.
- On any assumption about plants, the conclusion holds.
