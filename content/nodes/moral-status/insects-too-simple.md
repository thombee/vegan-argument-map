## The objection at full strength

An insect has a tiny fraction of the neurons a human has. Whatever is going on when a fly avoids a flame, it cannot be anything like a mammal's suffering. If we worried about every insect, nobody could walk across a lawn.

## Response

The size difference is real, and I would not claim a fly suffers like a dog. But the review by [Gibbons and colleagues](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/117533) is a good reason to stop assuming that small means empty. They found that adult flies, mosquitoes, cockroaches and termites meet six of eight criteria for pain, which they describe as strong evidence, and that most other adult orders meet three to four, described as substantial evidence. They found no good evidence that any insect failed a criterion.

That does not mean we can live without harming insects. Walking across a lawn is not the same as breeding billions for food. A decent line is to avoid harm where reasonably possible, and where the harm is the product, as with farmed animals, to avoid it.

It also means vegans should be cautious about enthusiasm for insect farming as the clean alternative. Whether insect farming is acceptable depends on exactly this question, and the evidence suggests it is not a safe assumption.

## In short

- Reviews find substantial or strong evidence of pain in many insects.
- Avoiding unavoidable harm is not the same as farming for food.
- 'Small' is not the same as 'empty'.
