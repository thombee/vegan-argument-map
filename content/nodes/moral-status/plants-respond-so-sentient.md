## The objection at full strength

Plants communicate through chemical and electrical signals, defend themselves against being eaten, and some experiments suggest learning. That is exactly what we would see in a being with a form of awareness that is different from ours.

## Response

Those findings are real, and I do not want to dismiss them. The question is what they are evidence of.

The plant biologists behind the [Trends in Plant Science paper](https://www.sci.news/biology/plant-consciousness-07359.html) argue that plant electrical signalling is genetically encoded and has been fine-tuned through generations of natural selection. In their view, responses of this kind are what we should expect from an organism that has evolved to survive without a nervous system, rather than signs of a mind. This is a live disagreement, not a closed case.

A useful test is to ask what evidence would separate a responsive but unfeeling organism from a feeling one. In animals we look at the brain structures involved, at behaviour that is flexible and costly to the animal, and at the evolutionary pressures that could explain feeling. On these tests the animal evidence points one way, and the plant evidence, so far, the other.

Even so, nothing here depends on certainty. If you think there is some chance plants matter, the best response is to eat plants directly, since animals eat many more of them.

## In short

- Responsiveness appears in organisms nobody thinks are conscious.
- Plant biologists argue the signalling is programmed by evolution, not evidence of a mind.
- If in doubt, eating plants directly uses fewer plants than feeding them to animals.
