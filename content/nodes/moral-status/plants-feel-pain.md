## The objection at full strength

Plants respond to damage. They release chemicals when eaten, send electrical signals, and some, like the mimosa, appear to learn. Some researchers talk about plant intelligence or plant neurobiology. We used to be sure animals could not feel and were wrong. How can vegans be confident plants do not?

## Response

It is reasonable to be curious, because plants are far more responsive than people tend to assume, and our confidence about minds has been wrong before.

But responding is not feeling. A group of plant biologists argued in *Trends in Plant Science* that plants neither possess nor require consciousness, as [reported here](https://www.sci.news/biology/plant-consciousness-07359.html). Their reasoning is that the brain structures thought necessary for consciousness are found only in vertebrates, arthropods and cephalopods, and that plant electrical signalling is genetically programmed and shaped by natural selection. This is one side of a debate, and I would not hide that. But on the evidence available there is no good reason to think a plant can suffer, while there is strong evidence about animals.

There is also an answer that does not depend on settling the debate. Animals eat plants, and a lot of them. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets) reports that around 80% of agricultural land is used for meat and dairy production, that 41% of the world's cereals go to animal feed, and that beef converts about 2% of feed energy into beef. If you were worried about plants, eating them directly would still be better than feeding them to animals first.

So whichever way the plant question goes, the conclusion holds.

## In short

- Responding to damage is not the same as feeling it.
- Plant biologists argue plants neither have nor need consciousness.
- Animal farming uses far more plants than eating them directly.
