## The objection at full strength

If a farm raises another animal to replace the one killed, and that animal also has a good life, the total happiness is the same. On a total view of well-being, no loss has occurred.

## Response

This is a recognised position, usually called the replaceability argument, and it follows from a purely total view of happiness. I will take it seriously.

The first difficulty is that it proves too much. If an individual's death is made up by someone else's birth, then killing a happy person is acceptable as long as someone is born to take their place. Almost nobody accepts that, because the harm falls on the person, who is not interchangeable.

The second is that even its defenders attach a high bar. The [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/) notes that painlessly killing a happy animal could be justified where people would otherwise go hungry. Where other food is available, that condition is not met.

For the related claim that these animals would not exist at all without farming, see [Farm animals wouldn't exist without us](/a/wouldnt-exist-otherwise/).

## In short

- Individuals are not interchangeable containers of happiness.
- The same logic would justify replacing a happy person.
- See the 'wouldn't exist otherwise' page for the related claim.
