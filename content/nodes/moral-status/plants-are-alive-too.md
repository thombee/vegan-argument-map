## The objection at full strength

The distinction between plant and animal is arbitrary. Both are living organisms with their own drive to survive, and all eating involves ending a life. Drawing a line at nervous systems is just a human preference.

## Response

Most people already draw a line somewhere inside life, and it is not arbitrary. Nobody thinks it is wrong to wash their hands, though bacteria die. The question is what makes ending a life matter, and the standard answer in the [philosophical literature](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/) is sentience: the capacity to experience things, so that things can go better or worse for the being.

A tree being felled may be a loss to an ecosystem and to us, and there are good reasons not to destroy plants carelessly. But on the best current evidence there is nobody there to whom it is happening, no point of view that is frightened or in pain. When an animal is confined and killed, there is.

Some philosophers do argue for a wider view, such as respect for all living things. Even on that view the quantity matters, and eating animals takes far more plant life than eating plants (see [Plants feel pain too](/a/plants-feel-pain/)).

## In short

- Nobody treats all life as equal; the question is what matters about it.
- Sentience is the capacity for things to go well or badly for someone.
- Even on wider views, animal farming uses more plant life, not less.
