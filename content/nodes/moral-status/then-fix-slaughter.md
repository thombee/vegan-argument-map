## The objection at full strength

If the problem is a flawed stunning method, the answer is to replace it. The committee itself recommends alternatives. Reform is more practical than asking everyone to change their diet.

## Response

Reform of cruel practices is worth supporting, and I would back the committee's recommendation. A vegan can campaign for better welfare while also not buying the product.

Two points qualify the answer. First, the same [opinion](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/opinion-on-carbon-dioxide-gas-stunning-of-pigs-and-alternative-methods/opinion-on-the-welfare-impacts-on-pigs-of-high-concentration-co2-gas-stunning-and-of-potential-alternative-stunning-methods) noted that there had been no significant alterations to systems deploying high-concentration CO2 since 2003, and recommended a transition of up to five years, so even a clear finding takes a long time to turn into change.

Second, a better stunning method only addresses one moment. It does not address whether the animal needs to die at all, which is the objection on the parent page, or the conditions of the life before it. For those, see [I only buy humane / free-range / local](/a/humane-meat/).

So reform and abstention are not alternatives. One reduces harm within the system; the other withdraws demand from it.

## In short

- Support better stunning and decline the product; they're compatible.
- Reform has been slow even where the finding is clear.
- Stunning only addresses the last moment, not the question of killing.
