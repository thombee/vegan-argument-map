## The objection at full strength

Every moral view allows partiality. You rescue your own child before a stranger's. Nations look after their citizens. Preferring humans to animals is the same thing at a larger scale. It is natural, universal and probably necessary for a society to function.

## Response

Partiality is real and I do not think morality asks us to give it up. A parent is allowed to prioritise their child, a friend their friend. The question is what justifies it.

In those cases the justification is a relationship, with history, care and obligations. Being the same species is not a relationship. It is a category, and the question is whether it carries moral weight by itself. The [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/) explains that "speciesism" is the view that only humans should be morally considered, and that the core argument against it is that species membership is morally irrelevant, in the way birthplace is.

Also notice the scale. Preferring your child in a rescue is a tie-break in an emergency. Eating animals is not a tie-break; it is causing serious harm to an animal to satisfy a preference when other food exists. That is a different order of cost.

"It is natural" is also not a justification. Many natural tendencies, including bias against other groups of humans, are ones we ask people to resist. Bentham's [footnote](https://www.econlib.org/library/Bentham/bnthPML18.html) asks what else could trace the line if not the capacity to suffer, and concludes that it is not reason or speech.

## In short

- Partiality rests on relationships, not on membership of a category.
- Preferring family is a tie-break; eating animals is causing serious harm for a preference.
- Being natural doesn't make a bias right.
