## The objection at full strength

You admit there is no precise line. So one person puts it at mammals, another at humans, another at anything with a face. Which is right? If there is no fact, my choice is as good as yours, and mine puts it at animals I don't eat.

## Response

There is a difference between a boundary that is blurry and a boundary that is wherever you want it. Blurry boundaries have a range of borderline cases and a clear region on either side. Pigs are not in the borderline zone; they are close to the clear cases, near dogs.

The test for a line is whether it is drawn for a reason that applies consistently. The [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/) records the core argument against speciesism: that species membership is morally irrelevant, in the way birthplace is. A line that places a dog on one side and a pig on the other has to say what relevant difference there is, and the usual candidates (intelligence, sociality, emotional life) do not obviously favour the dog.

If someone's real reason is "because I eat one and not the other", that is a description of habit, and a habit can be examined.

## In short

- Vague boundaries still have clear regions.
- A good line needs a reason applied consistently.
- Nothing obvious separates the pig from the dog.
