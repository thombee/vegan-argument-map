## The objection at full strength

Withdrawing from harm is something even a plant leaf or a single cell does. A fish thrashing on a line shows a reflex arc, not a suffering mind. You are reading human feeling into a mechanical response.

## Response

You are right to separate nociception, the detection of harm, from pain, the felt experience. Thrashing alone does not prove the latter, and that is why the better studies do not rely on it.

In [Sneddon and colleagues' trout experiments](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2003.2349), noxious substances were applied to the lips and the researchers measured what happened afterwards. Gill-cover beat rate rose, the fish took longer to resume feeding, and they showed unusual behaviours. Those effects lasted a long time, and the authors read this as a sign of discomfort. A simple reflex fires and finishes; this was a sustained change in what the animal did and how it behaved.

This is still evidence about behaviour, not a direct reading of experience, and the paper establishes nociception in fish and strongly suggests more. But the pattern matches what we accept as evidence of pain in mammals: ongoing change, reduced normal behaviour, and effects that are consistent with the animal being in a bad state.

If fish suffer here at all, it is not an invention of vegans; it is a result published in a major biology journal.

## In short

- Pain evidence needs more than thrashing; the trout studies measured lasting changes.
- Feeding and breathing stayed disrupted long after the stimulus.
- The same kind of evidence is accepted for mammals.
