## The objection at full strength

Ethics exists to regulate relations among people. It was created by humans, for humans' needs. To extend it to chickens is a category error, like holding a river to account.

## Response

The origin of morality does not settle its scope. Human beings invented mathematics for human purposes, and it applies to galaxies. What matters is what moral rules are for: preventing and limiting harm to those who can be harmed.

Most people already agree with that in practice. We think cruelty to a dog is wrong in itself, not just wrong because of what it does to us. The [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/) describes the sentience view: what makes a being morally considerable is that it can suffer, not that it is human. That is not a stretch of the human institution; it is what the institution rests on.

If morality were really just a human club, protecting infants who can't contribute would also be optional. Nobody believes that, which suggests we already recognise that the duty comes from the vulnerability of the one protected.

The extension to farm animals is therefore the application of a principle we already hold, in a place where we have been reluctant to apply it.

## In short

- Where a rule came from doesn't limit who it protects.
- We already condemn cruelty to dogs for the dog's sake.
- The principle that suffering matters is human morality's own.
