## The objection at full strength

Infants will become rational; people with severe disabilities are still members of a kind whose normal members reason. We owe them respect because of what they are, not what they can do at the moment. No pig belongs to that kind, so the argument from marginal cases fails.

## Response

This is a serious reply, and the [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/) lists linking marginal humans to a "rational nature" among the standard responses.

The difficulty is what the kind is doing. If the claim is that humans of low capacity matter because they are members of the human species, that is just species membership, and the question is why species should matter. That is the speciesism problem, taken up in [It's natural to prioritise your own species](/a/species-loyalty/). If the claim is about potential, it does not cover people who never had and never will have the capacity, such as a person with a profound disability from birth, and we do not think they matter less.

And to the extent the answer is that these humans are loved and valued by family, that makes them matter to us. It does not make them matter to themselves in some different way than a pig does. The moral question is about what the harm is to the one harmed.

## In short

- 'Kind membership' is species membership under another label.
- Potential doesn't cover those who never will have the capacity.
- See the speciesism page for the deeper question.
