## The objection at full strength

If an animal lives well and is killed instantly without knowing it, nothing bad has been experienced. Pain is what makes things bad, and there is none. Many serious philosophers have held this, and it is why people who care about animals still accept humane slaughter.

## Response

This is a position some utilitarians hold, and the [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/) notes that on that view a happy animal painlessly killed and eaten by people who would otherwise go hungry could be justified. It is notable how narrow that is: the people would otherwise go hungry. For most of us, who have other food, the interests are not comparable, since the animal's interest in not being killed is serious and ours in eating it is replaceable.

There are two more problems. The first is whether killing is painless in practice. In 2025 the UK government's Animal Welfare Committee [concluded](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/opinion-on-carbon-dioxide-gas-stunning-of-pigs-and-alternative-methods/opinion-on-the-welfare-impacts-on-pigs-of-high-concentration-co2-gas-stunning-and-of-potential-alternative-stunning-methods) that exposure to high concentrations of CO2 in commercial systems causes pigs to suffer pain, respiratory distress and fear, and recommended that the method be prohibited. So the premise that slaughter is painless should not be assumed.

The second is whether pain is the only harm. If a person were killed painlessly in their sleep we would still think it a serious wrong, because it takes away their future. Whether animals have a comparable interest in continued life is debated, and I will say plainly that it is less clear for animals than for people. But it is not obviously nothing, and where it is unclear, the risk of error falls on the one who loses their life.

## In short

- Slaughter is not reliably painless; a UK advisory body found high-CO2 stunning of pigs causes pain, distress and fear.
- Painless killing of a human is still a wrong, because of the future lost.
- Where the benefit is a replaceable meal, the burden of proof is on the killing.
