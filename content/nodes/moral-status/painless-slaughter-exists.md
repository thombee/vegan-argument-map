## The objection at full strength

Animals are meant to be stunned before they are killed, and skilled workers do this quickly. Much of the horror people imagine comes from older methods or from footage of the worst operators, not from the typical abattoir.

## Response

Regulation and good staff matter, and I do not want to paint every slaughterhouse as the worst one. Stunning is meant to make the animal insensible before the kill, and where it works it shortens suffering.

The question is whether it works for the main methods. The UK's Animal Welfare Committee, an advisory body to government, [concluded](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/opinion-on-carbon-dioxide-gas-stunning-of-pigs-and-alternative-methods/opinion-on-the-welfare-impacts-on-pigs-of-high-concentration-co2-gas-stunning-and-of-potential-alternative-stunning-methods) that exposure to high concentrations of CO2 in commercial systems causes pigs to suffer pain, respiratory distress and fear. It noted that since 2003 there had been no significant alterations to such systems, and recommended prohibiting the method, with a transition of no more than five years.

That is a conclusion about a commercial method, not about the worst operators. It shows that "approved" and "painless" can be far apart.

This opinion is about pigs and one stunning method, and I do not claim it describes every species or system. It is enough to show the premise needs evidence, not assumption.

## In short

- The UK advisory committee found high-CO2 stunning of pigs causes pain and fear.
- It said such systems had not changed significantly since 2003.
- 'Approved' and 'painless' are different things.
