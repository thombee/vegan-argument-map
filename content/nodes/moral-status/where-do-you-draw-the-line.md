## The objection at full strength

Veganism says animals matter because they are sentient. But sentience fades gradually: mammals, birds, fish, crustaceans, insects, worms, jellyfish, sponges. At some point you have to say which of them count. Any line you draw will look arbitrary, and if it is arbitrary the whole position is just one more preference.

## Response

The challenge is real, and I will not pretend there is a sharp line. But there does not have to be one for the clear cases to be clear.

Almost every important distinction is vague at the edges: adult and child, day and night, bald and not. Nobody concludes that adults and children are the same. A gradual boundary leaves the far ends where they are.

The [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/) says sentience cannot be observed directly, so there is real uncertainty with distant animals such as insects, and that several authors recommend erring on the side of caution there. In practice, decisions have been made. The UK's [Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/22/section/5) treats as animals any vertebrate other than humans, plus cephalopod molluscs and decapod crustaceans, with power to extend the list. That followed an [LSE review](https://www.lse.ac.uk/News/News-Assets/PDFs/2021/Sentience-in-Cephalopod-Molluscs-and-Decapod-Crustaceans-Final-Report-November-2021.pdf) which found very strong evidence of sentience in octopods and strong evidence in true crabs.

So the line follows the evidence, and the evidence is strongest for mammals and birds, the land animals we farm in the largest numbers. A person can stop at the clear cases, and be careful with the unclear ones, and still be consistent.

## In short

- Vague boundaries do not erase the clear cases.
- Caution where evidence is weaker is a rational policy, not an arbitrary one.
- Pigs, cows and chickens sit well inside any plausible line.
