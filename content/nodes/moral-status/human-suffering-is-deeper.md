## The objection at full strength

A human who is dying knows it, and fears for their family. A pig does not suffer in that dimension. So when interests conflict, human interests carry more weight, and that is not the same as saying animals don't matter at all.

## Response

I accept the first half. Where a real conflict exists, such as a medical decision, human interests with more depth can reasonably take priority, and few vegans claim otherwise.

The second half is where the argument stops working for food. The [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/) describes the weighing involved: interests can be crucial, important, replaceable or trivial, and a higher-ranked interest overrides a lower one. For most people in most places, eating meat is a replaceable interest, since other food is available. The animal's interest in not being confined, hurt and killed is not replaceable to the animal.

So the claim is not that a pig's life equals a human's in every respect. It is that a lesser claim on the human side, a meal, cannot outweigh a serious claim on the animal side, however you discount it.

## In short

- More capacity for suffering can justify priority in real conflicts.
- Food is usually a replaceable interest.
- A weaker human interest can't outweigh a serious animal interest.
