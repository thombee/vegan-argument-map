## The objection at full strength

Whatever the trophic number says, we do hunt and farm across ecosystems. If we stopped, populations would swing out of balance, and the food web we have shaped would suffer.

## Response

Animals bred for food are not part of a natural balance; they are a human creation. Our World in Data reports that [around 80% of agricultural land is used for meat and dairy](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets), so a shift away from them would mean far less land needed for farming, which leaves more room for wild animals and their predators, not less.

Where hunting is used for wildlife management, that is a specific argument about specific places, not a reason why most people buy meat in supermarkets. Wild populations being adjusted by people also doesn't answer why the diet itself should include meat. See also [Animals would overrun the world](/a/overpopulation/).

## In short

- Farmed animals are a human creation, not part of a natural balance.
- Cutting animal agriculture frees land and leaves more room for wildlife.
