## The objection at full strength

Humans are omnivores with a long history of eating animals. Meat-eating is found in a very wide range of cultures, and our bodies digest it without trouble. If something has been done for hundreds of thousands of years across every society, the objector says, it takes a lot to call it wrong. Veganism asks people to reject something that looks like a deep part of being human.

## Response

All of that is true, and none of it is the question. Veganism does not claim that humans cannot eat meat or never did. It claims that we should not kill and exploit animals when we have no need to.

"Natural" and "long-standing" do not tell us what is right. Infanticide, warfare and slavery have long histories in human societies. We judge them by their effects on those who suffer, and we should judge this the same way. The appeal to nature also proves too much: much of what people value, from medicine to shoes to farming itself, is not natural in the sense the objection needs.

The practical premise has changed too. For most of history, many people ate animals because food was scarce and there was no alternative. Today, the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics holds that [appropriately planned vegetarian, including vegan, diets are healthful and nutritionally adequate](https://digitalcommons.andrews.edu/pubs/648). That means the old necessity, where it existed, no longer holds for most people reading this. (Vegans do need a reliable B12 source; see [the B12 objection](/a/b12-unnatural/).)

So the real question is what we owe the animals when killing them is a choice rather than a requirement. The animal's capacity to suffer and its interest in staying alive do not depend on how long people have been ignoring them.

## In short

- Old and natural does not mean right; many long-standing practices are wrong.
- Veganism doesn't deny we can eat meat, only that we should when we don't have to.
- Major dietetic bodies say well-planned vegan diets are adequate, so the old necessity is gone for most people.
