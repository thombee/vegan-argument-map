## The objection at full strength

A cow gives hundreds of meals. A field of wheat harvest kills dozens of mice. Per meal, the cow might cause fewer deaths.

## Response

The reasoning counts deaths per meal but treats the cow's death as a single unit. A cow is one life with an interest in continuing, not a fixed-cost commodity. Meal arithmetic can justify almost any cruelty if the benefits can be spread widely enough.

It also leaves out what the cow eats. Across the world, [only about 12% of feed calories return to the human diet](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/8/3/034015) as meat and other animal products (Cassidy et al., 2013), so the meals from each animal come at a high cost in land and crops. Growing food for people directly can nourish more people per hectare.

If you want to reduce harm to animals, eating plants directly is the most reliable way to do it. If field deaths bother you, wildlife-friendly farming and buying from it are the answer, not eating a sentient animal.

## In short

- Meals per death treats a life as a unit of cost.
- Feed conversion means animal foods cost more land, not less.
