## The objection at full strength

Either animals have a right to life or they don't. If they do, we should protect them from predators. If we shouldn't, the right is empty and we're left with preference.

## Response

Rights come with different kinds of duties. The right not to be killed by us is a duty on us to refrain, which we can do. A right to be rescued from every threat is a much bigger claim, and no one holds it even for humans: we recognise a right not to be murdered, but few think the state must prevent every natural death.

Philosophers who defend animal rights disagree about how much we should do for wild animals, from leaving them alone to helping where we reliably can. On none of these views does it follow that we may kill animals for taste. The dispute is about how much we owe to wild animals, not about whether we may farm.

## In short

- Different rights bring different duties.
- Duty to refrain from harm doesn't imply a duty to police nature.
