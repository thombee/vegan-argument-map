## The objection at full strength

We are not separate from nature. We are primates with the same biology and appetites as other animals. Treating human meat-eating as a special moral problem, while excusing the lion, puts humans on an artificial pedestal.

## Response

We agree that humans are animals, and that is the point behind veganism: the animals we farm are fellow animals, with experiences that matter to them.

But "we are animals" cuts both ways. If our animal nature justified whatever we are inclined to do, it would justify theft, violence and much else. We do not accept that, because we are animals who can reflect, and reflection brings responsibility. The lion's behaviour is explained by biology alone. Ours is explained by biology plus choice.

Nor is this putting humans on a pedestal. It is the reverse: it asks us to apply to animals the consideration we already apply to each other, because they too can suffer. The claim that humans are just another predator is the claim that we have no special responsibilities, and few people actually believe that about themselves.

## In short

- Being animals explains our appetites; it doesn't justify acting on them.
- Humans can reflect, so we are accountable in a way a lion is not.
