## The objection at full strength

For Inuit and many other Indigenous peoples, hunting and fishing are not lifestyle choices. Food is expensive and hard to deliver in remote communities, and harvesting animals is tied to culture, identity, knowledge passed between generations and self-determination. Telling people whose food systems were disrupted by colonisation that they should adopt a diet suited to a supermarket can sound like a new form of colonial pressure. A philosophy that cannot answer this is not universal.

## Response

This objection deserves respect, and part of it is simply right.

The Vegan Society's definition of veganism seeks to exclude exploitation of animals [as far as is possible and practicable](https://vegansociety.com/go-vegan/definition-veganism). Where land-based food is the only realistic source of nutrition, or where cheap alternatives do not exist, the "practicable" part of that sentence matters. Inuit leaders describe high rates of food insecurity as [a public health crisis](https://itk.ca/inuit-nunangat-food-security-strategy-announcement/), and link harvesting country food to community ethics and Inuit identity. Veganism is not a demand that those communities abandon it. Decisions about their food belong to them, and supporting food sovereignty, including support for harvesters, is a better response than a lecture.

The honest conclusion is narrower than the objection supposes. The Inuit case shows that some people have real need and cultural claims. It does not show that everyone else does. Most people reading this live where affordable plant foods are available, and they are not making a survival choice or exercising Indigenous sovereignty when they buy meat. The existence of a hard case does not turn an easy one into a hard case.

Industrial animal farming is also quite a different thing from subsistence harvesting, and vegans have no need to treat them as the same.

## In short

- Veganism asks for what is possible and practicable; real need and food sovereignty matter.
- Respecting Inuit and other Indigenous food systems is consistent with veganism.
- A hard case elsewhere doesn't make buying meat in a supermarket necessary.
