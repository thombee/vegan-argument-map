## The objection at full strength

If you'd eat the fish on the island, your belief that killing is wrong is conditional. Then the question is only how much it costs, and that is a matter of degree, not principle.

## Response

Conditional is not the same as weak. A rule that gives way to survival is still a rule. Many serious commitments work this way: you don't lie, but you might to hide someone from a killer; you don't take property, but a starving person might.

The price of an exception matters. On the island, the cost of refusing is your life. In a supermarket it is a different recipe. Moving from "sometimes it's justified" to "so it's always justified" ignores the very feature, necessity, that makes the exception. See [Where do you draw the line?](/a/where-do-you-draw-the-line/) for how vegans think about edge cases.

## In short

- Conditional isn't the same as weak.
- The exception is defined by real need, which is rare.
