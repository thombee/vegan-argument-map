## The objection at full strength

Combines, ploughs and pesticides kill mice, rabbits, snakes and birds. Some philosophers, such as Steven Davis, have argued that a diet with grass-fed beef could cause fewer deaths than a diet of crops, because one cow feeds many people on land that doesn't need to be ploughed. If animal welfare matters to a vegan, the argument goes, the vegan has to count these deaths and cannot claim clean hands.

## Response

This is a genuine issue and vegans should not wave it away. Farming crops does kill and displace wild animals, and the number is not zero.

But the objection assumes that animal farming takes less cropland. It takes much more. Globally, [36% of the calories produced by the world's crops are used as animal feed, and only 12% of those feed calories end up in the human diet](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/8/3/034015) (Cassidy et al., 2013). Eating animals means growing crops, with their field deaths, and then feeding them to animals who are killed as well. Cutting out the middle step uses less land and sends fewer crops through the harvest.

The count itself is uncertain. Philosophers Bob Fischer and Andy Lamey estimated about 7.3 billion wild animal deaths a year in crop farming, but, as [Anthropocene Magazine reports](https://www.anthropocenemagazine.org/2018/07/how-many-animals-killed-in-agriculture/), the figure rests on thin evidence and they judged it clearly too high. They also see a possible future in which plant agriculture kills very few animals, perhaps none.

The conclusion for vegans is to reduce harm where possible: favour wildlife-friendly farming, and eat plants directly. It is not a reason to endorse killing farm animals that we do not need to kill.

## In short

- Field deaths are real, and vegans should say so.
- Animal farming needs far more cropland: 36% of crop calories go to feed.
- Eating plants directly avoids the extra crops and the animals killed for meat.
