## The objection at full strength

Lions, wolves and orcas kill other animals every day and nobody calls them wrong. Humans are animals too. If meat-eating is fine for them, why is it a moral failing for us?

## Response

Nobody is blaming the lion. A lion is an obligate carnivore: it cannot digest a plant diet and has no way of weighing the moral cost of what it does. Its killing is neither right nor wrong; it is simply what it is. That is why we do not put lions on trial.

Humans differ on both counts that matter. We can live well without eating animals, and we can reflect on what our choices do to others. Because we can choose, we are accountable in a way the lion is not. Using "the lion does it" as a defence also proves too much: some animals kill their young or their rivals, and nobody thinks that licenses humans to do so.

If you grant that animals can suffer, the question becomes whether we should cause suffering and death we do not need to cause. Pointing at an animal that has no alternative does not answer it.

(For the related argument that we should then stop predation in the wild, see [Animals kill each other in the wild](/a/animals-eat-each-other/).)

## In short

- Lions can't live on plants and can't weigh moral reasons; humans can do both.
- Being able to choose is what makes us accountable.
- 'Animals do it' would also excuse things we plainly reject.
