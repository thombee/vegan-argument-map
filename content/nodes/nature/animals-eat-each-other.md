## The objection at full strength

Nature is full of killing. Predators eat prey, parasites eat hosts, and most animals die painfully. If that is the natural order, then killing animals for food is just taking part in it. And if vegans think killing is wrong, they should be trying to stop it everywhere.

## Response

There are two answers, one for each half.

The first is about taking part. What animals do in the wild is not a moral standard, because most of them are not moral agents. We aren't comparing ourselves with a lion that has no other option; we are comparing ourselves with who we want to be. The fact that nature includes killing doesn't make killing a model for us, any more than it makes other things in nature a model. See also [Lions eat meat](/a/lions-eat-meat/).

The second is about stopping it. Vegans mostly hold that we have a strong duty not to cause harm and a weaker, more complicated duty to prevent harm we did not cause. Not eating animals is within our power and is clearly our responsibility. Rewriting ecosystems is neither within our power nor clearly good, and interfering can do more damage than it prevents. That is a reason for humility about wild predation, not a reason to take part in farming.

## In short

- Nature is not a moral model.
- Not causing harm is our duty; stopping all harm in the wild is a separate, harder question.
