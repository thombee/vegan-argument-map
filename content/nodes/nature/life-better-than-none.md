## The objection at full strength

Imagine a pasture-raised animal with a good life and a quick death. Compared with never existing, the animal gains. If you can't point to suffering, then you can't say the animal was wronged.

## Response

That is the best case, and it deserves a fair answer. If a life is very good, that is a good thing. But two points remain.

One is that the best case is a separate debate; see [I only buy humane / free-range / local](/a/humane-meat/).

The other is that even in the best case, the ending matters. According to [Browning and Veit](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7278393), death harms by taking away the animal's future experiences, which is why they conclude no slaughter can be fully humane. If a good life is a good thing, cutting it short is a loss to the animal. "She had a good life" is a reason to be glad about the past and a reason not to end the future.

## In short

- The best case is a separate debate.
- Even a good life is cut short; that is a loss to the animal.
