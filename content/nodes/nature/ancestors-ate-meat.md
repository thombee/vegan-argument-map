## The objection at full strength

Evolution shaped our digestion, our teeth, and our nutritional needs around a diet that included animal foods. If we are adapted to meat, then removing it should cost us something, and the people who struggle on vegan diets are what you would expect.

## Response

There is some truth here. Meat and animal foods have been part of the human diet for a very long time, and some nutrients, B12 especially, are not reliably found in plant foods. A vegan has to plan for that with fortified foods or supplements.

But "we evolved eating X" has never meant "we need X today". Evolution selects for survival and reproduction in past environments, not for what is best now or what is right. Humans are flexible omnivores precisely because we can thrive on very different diets in different parts of the world, including diets very low in animal products.

Whether a modern vegan diet works is an empirical question, and the main dietetic body in the United States has answered it: the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics says [appropriately planned vegetarian, including vegan, diets are healthful and nutritionally adequate](https://digitalcommons.andrews.edu/pubs/648). Planning matters, and individual cases differ, so anyone changing diet should read about nutrients and consider advice from a dietitian.

Finally, even if a species were evolved to eat meat, that would not make killing a sentient animal morally fine. It would only describe our history.

## In short

- Evolving with meat shows what we can eat, not what we must eat.
- The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics says well-planned vegan diets are adequate.
- Supplement B12 and plan your diet; see the health pages for detail.
