## The objection at full strength

Looking after billions of animals for their natural lives would be extraordinarily expensive, and nobody would fund it. So culling and eating them is the only realistic course.

## Response

The cost question is real, and nobody has a neat answer. But two things help. First, the cost is a one-time transition cost that shrinks every year, because fewer animals are bred as demand falls; the objection pictures all of today's animals existing at once, which would not happen. Second, a practical difficulty is a reason to plan well, not a reason to keep producing animals at the current scale. We would not accept "it is expensive to stop" as a reason to continue other harms.

## In short

- The cost shrinks as breeding falls.
- Practical difficulty argues for a plan, not for continuing.
