## The objection at full strength

Once you allow exceptions for need or culture, the line is blurry. I have my own culture, a limited budget and a family that won't change. By the same logic, I'm also exempt.

## Response

Exceptions are not a free pass, and the question is the right one to ask honestly. "Possible and practicable" is a test about real alternatives: [that is the wording in the Vegan Society definition](https://vegansociety.com/go-vegan/definition-veganism). Someone with a medical condition that restricts food, a person living in a food desert, or someone without cooking facilities may truly have fewer options.

For most people the question is whether they have an alternative at all, and most do. Preference, habit and a family that won't change are real pressures. They are also not the same as lacking food. Moving is hard; it is not the same as being impossible.

If you are in a situation where you truly cannot, vegans generally say do what you can. See [It's too hard / inconvenient](/a/too-hard/) for how to approach it.

## In short

- The exemption follows real need, not preference.
- Most people have alternatives; being hard is not being impossible.
