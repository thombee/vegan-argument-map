## The objection at full strength

If everyone went vegan, billions of cows, pigs and chickens would never be born. A life, even a short one, is better than no life. Farmers bring animals into existence, feed them and protect them from predators, so it can be argued that they are doing the animals a favour. Ending demand would deprive them of the chance to live at all.

## Response

The strongest version of this argument says existence is a benefit. But there is no animal waiting in a queue to be born who is harmed if it never is. Not creating a being is not a harm to that being, since there is nobody who loses out. Veganism takes nothing from anyone who exists.

For animals who do exist, the question is whether the life we give is a favour or a cost. Death itself matters, because it ends the animal's future: [researchers writing in Animals argue that death harms an animal by depriving it of future good experiences, and conclude that no slaughter can be completely humane](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7278393) (Browning & Veit, 2020). The numbers are also huge: FAO data compiled by Our World in Data show about [73.8 billion chickens and 1.4 billion pigs slaughtered in 2021](https://ourworldindata.org/how-many-animals-get-slaughtered-every-day).

Finally, notice the structure. "I brought you into existence, so I may use you" would be rejected for children, dogs or people. We usually think that bringing a being into existence adds duties to it, not rights over it.

## In short

- Not-yet-existing animals aren't harmed by never being born.
- Creating a life doesn't give us the right to end it.
- Death deprives an animal of its future.
