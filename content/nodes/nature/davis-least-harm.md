## The objection at full strength

Davis's argument is that cattle grazing on pasture need no ploughing, and one cow provides hundreds of meals with one deliberate death. A vegan diet requires harvesting machinery, which kills many small animals. By the numbers, the grass-fed diet looks like the least-harm option.

## Response

The argument is clever and it has been taken seriously by philosophers on both sides, but it has problems.

First, it counts only some land. Our World in Data reports that [around 80% of agricultural land is used for meat and dairy](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets) which includes pasture as well as feed crops. Pasture beef doesn't remove the land question; it moves it.

Second, the death figures are soft. According to [reporting on the work of Fischer and Lamey](https://www.anthropocenemagazine.org/2018/07/how-many-animals-killed-in-agriculture/), the estimate of wild deaths from crops relies on thin data and they judged it clearly too high.

Third, there is a moral difference between a death that is an unintended side effect and a death that is the very purpose of the practice. Both matter, but choosing to kill a cow for lunch is a different act from accepting a risk to field animals while growing food. That difference is why many people accept that reducing the second does not justify the first.

## In short

- Pasture beef still needs a deliberate killing and a lot of land.
- The field-death estimates are uncertain, and the authors themselves judge them too high.
- Intended and unintended deaths differ morally.
