## The objection at full strength

In nature, big predators sit at the top and eat those below them. Humans have no natural enemies and have always hunted what we like. Eating animals is simply our place in the order of things.

## Response

It is a familiar phrase, but it is not accurate. Researchers who calculated the trophic level of humans from national food-supply data found that the global average is 2.21, [the same as anchoveta, a small fish](https://faunalytics.org/eating-up-the-worlds-food-web-and-the-human-trophic-level/) (Bonhommeau et al., 2013, as summarised by Faunalytics), well below true apex predators. We are omnivores who eat a lot of plants. That figure has risen as diets include more meat, which is a change in what we choose rather than a fixed rank.

More importantly, "top of the food chain" describes power, not permission. Being able to dominate other species does not give us a right to; the ability to do something is not a reason for doing it. If it were, any strong party would be justified in using any weaker one.

You can accept the ecology and still ask the moral question: given that we are able to live well without it, why take the lives of animals that want to live?

## In short

- The human trophic level is about 2.21, like an anchovy, not an apex predator.
- Being able to dominate is not the same as being entitled to.
