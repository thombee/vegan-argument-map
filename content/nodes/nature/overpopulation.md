## The objection at full strength

There are billions of farm animals. If everyone went vegan tomorrow, what would happen to them? They can't be released into the wild, and they'd breed, overrun the countryside and destroy habitats. Eating them is how we keep numbers in check.

## Response

This pictures farm animals as wild populations that need culling. They are not. They exist in these numbers because people breed them on purpose to meet demand. Our World in Data, using FAO figures, reports that in 2021 about [73.8 billion chickens, 1.4 billion pigs and 332 million cattle were slaughtered](https://ourworldindata.org/how-many-animals-get-slaughtered-every-day). Those numbers are the result of deliberate breeding, and they fall when people stop buying, since producers breed fewer animals.

So the question of "what to do with the animals" is really a question about a transition. No one proposes opening every barn on the same day. The change is gradual; fewer animals are bred each year, and animals already alive live out their lives on farms or in sanctuaries.

The argument also gets the causation backwards: it is the practice of breeding animals for food that creates the large populations.

## In short

- Farm animal numbers are set by breeding to meet demand.
- Falling demand means fewer births; nobody proposes releasing every animal at once.
