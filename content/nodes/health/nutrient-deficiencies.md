## The objection at full strength

This is a fair objection and it is backed by data. Systematic reviews find that vegans, on average, eat less of several nutrients, including B12, vitamin D, iodine, zinc, calcium and selenium, and a German study found one-third of the vegans it tested had iodine levels below the WHO threshold for severe deficiency. Omega-3 fats from fish, calcium from dairy and iron from meat are all hard to replace. If a diet leaves you short of that many things, the argument goes, it is a risky diet.

## Response

The shortfalls are real, and any honest vegan or dietitian will name them. The [systematic review by Bakaloudi and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2020.11.035) found low average intakes of B12, vitamin D, iodine, zinc, calcium and selenium. [Dietitians Australia](https://member.dietitiansaustralia.org.au/Common/Uploaded%20files/DAA/Resource_Library/2020/VF_A_Guide_to_Vegan_Eating.pdf) says calcium, B12, long-chain omega-3s and vitamin D can be hard to get on a vegan diet and that vegans might also need to supplement calcium, iron or omega-3. The [British Dietetic Association](https://www.bda.uk.com/resource/vegetarian-vegan-plant-based-diet.html) flags iodine as a risk and cautions against eating seaweed more than once a week. The German study, [Weikert et al.](https://doi.org/10.3238/arztebl.2020.0575), found markedly lower iodine excretion in vegans (small sample, 36 per group).

But the same review found vegan diets were not linked with deficiencies in vitamins A, B1, B6, C, E, iron, magnesium, copper or folate, and noted that low intakes are not always associated with health impairments. The professional position is that a well-planned vegan diet works: the [NHMRC](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/australian-dietary-guidelines-2013.pdf) says appropriately planned vegan diets are healthy and nutritionally adequate, and the [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025) agrees, adding that vegans need a reliable B12 source.

In practice, that means a B12 supplement or fortified foods, an iodine source such as iodised salt, calcium-set tofu or fortified plant milks, vitamin D, and attention to omega-3. Nobody should be left with the impression that this is automatic. If you are unsure, a dietitian can check your plan and a GP can order blood tests.

## In short

- Several nutrients need attention: B12 (always), iodine, vitamin D, calcium, omega-3, zinc, and sometimes iron.
- The gaps are real in studies, and so are the fixes.
- Dietetic bodies still say planned vegan diets are adequate; see a dietitian if unsure.
