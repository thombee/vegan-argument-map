## The objection at full strength

If major bodies said vegan diets were safe for children, they would agree. They do not. Several paediatric associations warn against it. That alone should make any parent hesitate, whatever a dietetic association says.

## Response

The disagreement is real, and it deserves a fair account. A [narrative review of position papers](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15224715) describes a spectrum: some associations support well-designed vegan diets for children, with careful planning, B12 supplementation and regular medical and dietetic oversight, while specific paediatric associations caution against them, citing potential harm and a lack of adequate evidence. The authors argue that some of the criticisms lean on lower-quality or outdated studies, or on children whose vegan diet was not well designed, and that a fair comparison would assess well-designed diets of both kinds. They call for an updated literature review.

The German Nutrition Society shows how evidence limits translate into positions. In [2024 it stopped advising against](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/gesund-und-gruen-148427/) the diet for children and pregnancy, but did not endorse it either, citing improved but still limited data and the need for professional guidance.

We cannot tell you which body will be proven right, and we would not pretend otherwise. If you are weighing this for a child, the safest path is the one on which these bodies converge: do not do it without B12 and professional support. The ethical case is unchanged either way, as it concerns what we do to animals.

## In short

- Position papers differ, and the cautious ones often cite older or lower-quality studies.
- Evidence is still limited, so certainty on either side is unwarranted.
- Where they agree: B12 supplements and professional support.
