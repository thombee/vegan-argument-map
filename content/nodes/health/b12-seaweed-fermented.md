## The objection at full strength

People in the vegan community sometimes say you can get B12 from nori, fermented foods, spirulina, or soil on unwashed vegetables, so supplements are optional. If that were true, the "you must supplement" message would be exaggerated.

## Response

Some plant foods do contain B12, and that is where the idea comes from. A [review of B12 sources and bioavailability](https://doi.org/10.3181/0703-mr-67) notes that dried laver (nori) contains substantial B12. It also states that other edible algae contained none or only traces, and that edible blue-green algae sold as supplements mostly contain pseudovitamin B12, which is inactive in humans, so they are not suitable B12 sources for vegans.

A food can contain B12 and still not be a dependable source. Amounts vary with product and batch, and relying on a single food, or on traces on vegetables, is exactly how deficiency happens. The [British Dietetic Association](https://www.bda.uk.com/resource/vegetarian-vegan-plant-based-diet.html) says the only reliable sources for vegans are fortified foods and supplements. The [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025) says the same.

So the useful advice is simple: take a B12 supplement or eat fortified foods regularly, and ask your doctor about a blood test if you are unsure. Nori and the rest are a bonus, not a plan. If a supplement is a nuisance, note that it is cheap and does the job.

## In short

- Nori contains some B12, but other algae contain little and spirulina-type supplements contain an inactive form.
- Soil, fermented foods and unwashed vegetables are not dependable sources.
- Use fortified foods or a supplement, as every dietetic body says.
