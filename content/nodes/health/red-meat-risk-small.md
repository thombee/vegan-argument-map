## The objection at full strength

The headline that bacon is in the same category as cigarettes was misleading. The absolute risk added by a daily sausage is small. So people shouldn't panic about meat.

## Response

You are right that the classification is often misread, and WHO says so itself. Its [question-and-answer page](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/cancer-carcinogenicity-of-the-consumption-of-red-meat-and-processed-meat) explains that the categories indicate the strength of evidence as to whether a substance is capable of causing cancer, but do not measure the likelihood that cancer will occur at a particular level of exposure. Processed meat is in Group 1 because there is sufficient evidence in humans, and red meat in Group 2A because the evidence is more limited. Sharing a category does not mean sharing the same level of risk.

The same page also notes that meat can be part of a healthy diet and provides nutrients that are important for good health, and that many national guidelines recommend limiting processed and red meat because higher intakes are linked to heart disease, type 2 diabetes and some cancers.

So we agree: nobody should panic, and we do not argue that a bacon sandwich will give you cancer. The honest summary is that the health data on meat show some risks that grow with higher intakes, that we are not claiming the risk to any one person is large, and that this is not our main reason for avoiding it. The strongest case is about the animals.

## In short

- The IARC category shows strength of evidence, not size of risk; WHO says so.
- Processed meat is Group 1 and red meat Group 2A, and risks rise with intake.
- We don't claim meat is deadly; our main argument is about animals.
