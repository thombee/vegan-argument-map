## The objection at full strength

Children and pregnant women have the highest nutritional needs and the least room for error. Growth, brain development and bone building depend on B12, iron, iodine, DHA, calcium and energy, and some of these are hard to get without animal foods. Some paediatric societies advise against vegan diets for children altogether, and there are well-publicised cases of vegan infants who became seriously unwell. A cautious person would say: do not experiment on a child.

## Response

This is the objection to take most seriously, and the professional bodies do not all agree. On one side, the [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025) says appropriately planned vegan diets are appropriate for pregnancy, lactation, infancy, childhood and adolescence, and the [Australian Dietary Guidelines](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/australian-dietary-guidelines-2013.pdf) say well-planned vegetarian diets, including vegan, suit all stages of life. On the other, a [review of position papers](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15224715) found that some associations support well-designed vegan diets for children while others caution against them. Germany's DGE used to advise against vegan diets for these groups; in [2024 it moved](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/gesund-und-gruen-148427/) to making neither a recommendation for nor against, citing improved but still limited data, and stressing professional counselling because poor planning can have partly irreversible consequences.

Everyone agrees on the practical bit. Supplement B12, plan for iodine, vitamin D, calcium, iron and energy, and have the child's growth monitored by a doctor or dietitian. Done carelessly, the diet can harm a child; done with planning and monitoring, major bodies say it can support healthy growth. A parent doing this should not rely on social media or a blog, but on a paediatric dietitian.

None of this is medical advice. Whether a child should be fed this way is a decision for the family with qualified help. The moral argument for veganism does not depend on any individual child's diet.

## In short

- Dietetic bodies are split: some say planned vegan diets suit all life stages, others advise caution.
- Everyone agrees on supplementing B12 and planning iodine, vitamin D, calcium, iron and energy.
- Get a paediatric dietitian's help; do not wing it.
