## The objection at full strength

Vegans do develop B12 deficiency. The documented cases are real: anaemia, nerve damage, babies harmed when their mothers did not supplement. If a diet routinely produces deficiency in people who follow it, the objection goes, the diet is the problem and not the individuals.

## Response

Some vegans are deficient, and the reason is mostly that they do not take B12. The [systematic review by Bakaloudi and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2020.11.035) found vegan B12 intakes from food of only 0.24 to 0.49 micrograms a day against a recommendation of 2.4, so food alone does not do it. By contrast, in a [German study of 36 vegans and 36 omnivores](https://doi.org/10.3238/arztebl.2020.0575), B12 markers were similar in both groups even though the vegans ate very little dietary B12, probably because nearly all of them were using supplements. It is a small study, but it shows what supplementing does.

Deficiency matters. A [2026 review](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2026.1898533) describes how it can show up in the blood, the nervous system and the skin, and that these effects are largely preventable with timely diagnosis and treatment. That is a reason to be diligent, and to test if you have symptoms.

It also shows the diet is not unsafe so much as unforgiving of one specific omission. Meat-eaters also develop B12 deficiency, for example through absorption problems, and nobody concludes their diet is unsuitable. What the evidence supports is: supplement, test occasionally, and see a doctor if you feel unwell.

## In short

- Vegan B12 deficiency is real and almost always preventable with supplements or fortified foods.
- Where vegans supplement, B12 markers can match omnivores.
- It is an argument for supplementing, not against the diet.
