## The objection at full strength

Muscle is made of protein, and the best protein comes from animals. The classic picture of a strong athlete is someone eating chicken, eggs, whey and steak. Plant proteins are digested less well, and vegans have lower iron and B12, both important for oxygen delivery and energy. You may be healthy on a vegan diet, but not at the top of your sport.

## Response

The evidence does not back the strong version of this claim. A [systematic review of eight studies](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2015-0231) (seven randomised trials and one cross-sectional) that directly compared vegetarian-based and omnivorous diets and assessed actual performance found no distinguished differences. A [12-week resistance training trial](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01434-9) that matched protein at 1.6 g per kg per day found vegans and omnivores gained lean mass, muscle size and strength alike. In [recreational runners](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-019-0289-4), maximal power output and lactate profiles were similar in vegan, vegetarian and omnivorous groups. The [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025) lists athletes among those for whom appropriately planned vegan diets are suitable.

Limits worth stating: these studies are small and mostly of recreational athletes, young men or short periods, so they say little about elite performance over years. A meta-analysis discussed under [But animal protein builds more muscle](/a/plant-protein-builds-less-muscle/) found a small lean-mass advantage for animal protein in younger adults. Athletes need to attend to total energy, protein, iron and B12.

We are not claiming a vegan diet improves performance; the claim is that the evidence does not show it prevents good performance. A sports dietitian can tailor a plan.

## In short

- Controlled trials and a systematic review find no consistent performance gap between vegan and omnivorous diets.
- Protein-matched vegans gained muscle as well as omnivores in a 12-week trial.
- Watch total energy, protein, iron and B12; a sports dietitian can help.
