## The objection at full strength

Large studies link high ultra-processed food intake with heart disease and early death. If vegan food is more processed, as your own sources say, the benefits of a plant-based diet must be cancelled out.

## Response

That is a fair challenge, and the evidence to hand is mixed. A recent [UK Biobank cohort of nearly 125,000 adults](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2026.101736) looked at plant-based diet patterns with high and low ultra-processed food content. Higher adherence to a healthy plant-based pattern was linked with 8 to 28 percent lower all-cause mortality and type 2 diabetes whether the pattern was high or low in ultra-processed foods, and the authors suggest overall diet quality may matter more than processing level. For cardiovascular disease, the high-UPF healthy pattern showed an 11 percent lower risk while the low-UPF one showed no clear association.

Some caveats. These were observational data, and the study measured plant-based dietary patterns, not people who are fully vegan. The French study by [Gehring and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1093/jn/nxaa196) raised the opposite concern, that ultra-processed food could undermine the benefits of vegetarian diets. Nobody has settled this.

Our position is modest: eat mostly whole and minimally processed plant foods, use the processed vegan products in moderation, and do not expect a vegan label to protect you from a poor diet. For the question of whether to use animals for food, health data on ultra-processed foods do not change the answer.

## In short

- Ultra-processed food is linked to poor health, and vegan diets are not exempt.
- A large UK cohort found healthy plant-based patterns linked with lower mortality regardless of UPF content.
- Eat mostly whole foods; the evidence is not settled.
