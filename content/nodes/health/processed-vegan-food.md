## The objection at full strength

Walk down a supermarket aisle: vegan nuggets, vegan sausages, vegan cheese made from starch and coconut oil, and a long list of additives. Plenty of vegan products are ultra-processed foods, which are linked to poor health. A diet built on them might technically contain no animal products, but it is not a healthy one.

## Response

There is truth here. Vegan does not mean healthy: chips, soft drink and lollies are vegan. A French study, [Gehring and colleagues (NutriNet-Santé)](https://doi.org/10.1093/jn/nxaa196), found that the more people avoided animal foods, the larger the share of their energy came from ultra-processed foods: 33.0% for meat-eaters, 37.0% for vegetarians and 39.5% for vegans. The authors concluded that not all vegetarian diets necessarily have health benefits, and that people who had been vegetarian for a shorter time or started younger ate more ultra-processed food.

The same study also scored overall plant-based diet quality, and vegans came out highest (67.9 on a healthy plant-based diet index versus 53.5 for meat-eaters). So processed food and an otherwise high-quality plant diet can go together. Whole foods are what the [Australian Dietary Guidelines](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/australian-dietary-guidelines-2013.pdf) emphasise for vegans: legumes, nuts, seeds and wholegrains as valuable sources of protein, iron and zinc.

The practical lesson is to build a vegan diet around beans, lentils, tofu, grains, vegetables and fruit, and treat the nuggets and sausages as occasional extras. How healthy plant-based meats are is a live debate (see below). For the moral argument, however, it does not matter: a vegan diet of lentils and rice is entirely possible, and processed vegan foods only need to be compared fairly with processed meat, which the [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/cancer-carcinogenicity-of-the-consumption-of-red-meat-and-processed-meat) classes as carcinogenic.

## In short

- Vegan is not automatically healthy: ultra-processed vegan food exists and is common.
- In one study vegans got more UPF energy but scored higher on overall plant-diet quality.
- A diet of lentils, rice, vegetables and tofu involves no ultra-processed food.
