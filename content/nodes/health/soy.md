## The objection at full strength

Soy contains isoflavones, plant compounds that can bind to oestrogen receptors, and you can find alarming case reports of men with breast growth or low testosterone after eating large amounts of soy. Soy is also heavily processed into isolates and protein powders that vegans lean on, and it is in a lot of fake meat. Why take the risk?

## Response

This is one of the best-studied questions in nutrition, and the clinical evidence does not support the worry. A [2021 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.reprotox.2020.12.019) pooled 41 clinical studies in adult men eating soy foods, soy protein or isoflavone extracts, and found no significant effects on total or free testosterone, oestradiol or oestrone, whatever the dose or study duration. An earlier [2010 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fertnstert.2009.04.038) reached the same conclusion for testosterone, SHBG and free androgen index.

The case reports that circulate are real, but a case report cannot show that soy was the cause, and it cannot outweigh controlled trials covering well over a thousand men. We should say that trials are limited in length and cannot cover every extreme intake, so the sensible approach is ordinary servings, not litres a day.

Soy is a useful food, not a requirement. The [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025) lists soy products among the vegan diet's cholesterol-lowering, glucose-friendly staples, and dietitians list tofu, tempeh and soy milk as protein and calcium sources. If you have a thyroid condition or take medication, ask your doctor about soy; the research above is about healthy men's hormones. Concerns about soy farming and forests are a separate issue, covered at [Soy destroys the rainforest](/a/soy-deforestation/).

## In short

- Over 40 clinical studies show no effect of soy or isoflavones on male testosterone or oestrogen.
- Case reports cannot outweigh controlled trials.
- Soy is a useful food but not essential.
