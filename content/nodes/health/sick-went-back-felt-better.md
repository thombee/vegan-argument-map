## The objection at full strength

I got better within days of eating meat. The simplest explanation is that my body needed it. Why should I assume anything else?

## Response

It is a reasonable inference, and it is one of the possible explanations. Meat delivers B12, easily absorbed iron, zinc and plenty of energy and protein in a compact form. If your diet was short of any of them, adding meat could well have helped. A [meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1080/10408398.2016.1259210) found lower iron stores in vegetarians on average, and a [2026 review](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2026.1898533) describes the many ways B12 deficiency can present.

Where we would be careful is the leap from "meat fixed it" to "I need meat". The same gap could have been closed by a B12 supplement, iron-rich plant foods with vitamin C, more calories or fortified foods, and a blood test would have shown which was the issue. Other explanations are possible too, and we cannot know which applies to you.

If you are curious, a doctor can check B12, ferritin, vitamin D and thyroid markers. If you have returned to eating meat and are comfortable, that is your decision. See [Meat is healthy and nutrient-dense](/a/meat-is-healthy/) for the wider claims.

## In short

- Feeling better on meat is consistent with fixing a B12, iron or energy gap.
- The same gap can usually be fixed without animals, and a blood test shows which it was.
- We cannot tell which applies to you; a doctor can.
