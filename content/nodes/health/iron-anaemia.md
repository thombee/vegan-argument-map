## The objection at full strength

Meat contains haem iron, which the body absorbs more easily than the iron in plants, and plant foods contain compounds that block absorption. Women of childbearing age lose iron monthly. So vegans, especially vegan women and endurance athletes, are at higher risk of anaemia.

## Response

There is a real signal here. A [meta-analysis of 24 cross-sectional studies](https://doi.org/10.1080/10408398.2016.1259210) found adult vegetarians have significantly lower serum ferritin (the iron store marker) than non-vegetarians, with the largest gap in men. Plant iron is absorbed less efficiently, and the [Australian Dietary Guidelines](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/australian-dietary-guidelines-2013.pdf) state that animal foods are highly bioavailable sources of iron, calcium and B12, so care is needed to include alternatives.

Lower stores are not the same as anaemia, though. The [Bakaloudi review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2020.11.035) found vegan diets were not related to deficiencies in iron. In a small [German comparison](https://doi.org/10.3238/arztebl.2020.0575) iron deficiency showed up in four of 36 vegans and three of 36 omnivores. In a study of [recreational runners](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu11051146), fewer than 30% of any group had depleted iron stores, and no one had iron-deficiency anaemia. These samples are small, and individuals vary a great deal.

What helps: legumes, tofu, tempeh, nuts, seeds, fortified cereals, and eating vitamin C-rich foods with meals while keeping tea and coffee away from them, as [Dietitians Australia advises](https://member.dietitiansaustralia.org.au/Common/Uploaded%20files/DAA/Resource_Library/2020/VF_A_Guide_to_Vegan_Eating.pdf). If you feel unusually tired, ask your GP for a ferritin test rather than guessing or supplementing without testing.

## In short

- Vegetarians have lower iron stores on average; it is a real thing to watch.
- Studies of vegans find anaemia no more common than in omnivores, though the samples are small.
- Vitamin C with meals helps; test ferritin if you feel flat.
