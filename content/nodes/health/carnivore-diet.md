## The objection at full strength

Plenty of people report that cutting everything but meat ended migraines, joint pain, gut problems, depression or weight gain after other diets failed. Their experience is real: they felt unwell, they changed what they ate, and they felt better. A large survey of carnivore dieters found high satisfaction and few adverse symptoms. If that is what worked, who are you to say it didn't?

## Response

We take people's experience seriously. If you were sick, are now well, and the carnivore diet is the thing that changed, that is meaningful to you and we do not doubt it.

The limit is what it can show for anyone else. The biggest data set is a [social media survey of 2029 adults](https://doi.org/10.1093/cdn/nzab133) who had eaten this way for at least six months. It found high satisfaction and few adverse symptoms, but also high LDL cholesterol (median 172 mg/dL among those who reported it), and the authors say its generalisability and the long-term effects need study. A survey of people who stayed on a diet leaves out those who tried and stopped. A [2026 scoping review](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18020348) found only nine human studies, with small samples, short durations and no control groups. It reports possible short-term benefits such as weight loss and better satiety, alongside risks of deficiencies (vitamins C and D, calcium, magnesium, iodine, fibre) and raised LDL, and concludes that long-term adherence cannot be recommended.

A diet that changes everything at once could help for reasons other than the one you credit, and only a controlled trial can sort out which. Meat itself is also not risk-free: the [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/cancer-carcinogenicity-of-the-consumption-of-red-meat-and-processed-meat) lists processed meat as carcinogenic and red meat as probably carcinogenic to humans. If you have an autoimmune or gut condition, a doctor or dietitian can design an elimination approach that does not depend on one food group.

## In short

- Personal recovery stories are real but cannot show the diet was the cause.
- The best evidence is a self-selected survey and a scoping review of nine small studies; long-term safety is unproven.
- Talk to a doctor or dietitian about elimination approaches.
