## The objection at full strength

Pregnancy increases the need for iron, iodine, B12, folate, choline and DHA, nutrients that are often scarce on a vegan diet. A shortfall can affect the baby's brain development in ways that cannot be reversed. It seems irresponsible to take that chance for a dietary choice.

## Response

The nutrients are the right ones to worry about. A [2019 review of vegetarian and vegan diets in pregnancy](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu11030557) lists protein, iron, vitamin D, calcium, iodine, omega-3 and B12 as the places where plant-based diets risk deficiency. It nonetheless concludes that well-planned vegetarian and vegan diets may be considered safe during pregnancy and lactation, but "require a strong awareness for a balanced intake of key nutrients", and recommends preparing nutrition before conception to avoid maternal undernutrition. The review is based on observational human studies.

The [British Dietetic Association](https://www.bda.uk.com/resource/vegetarian-vegan-plant-based-diet.html) flags iodine as a risk on plant-based diets and recommends speaking to a dietitian before taking an iodine supplement. The [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025) says appropriately planned vegan diets are appropriate in pregnancy and lactation, with reliable B12 sources.

So the answer is "planning, not prohibition". Anyone pregnant or trying to be, vegan or not, should be under the care of a midwife or doctor and ideally a dietitian, with blood tests for B12, iron and vitamin D. The risk comes from improvised diets, and the fix is professional support. If you have symptoms or concerns, see your doctor.

## In short

- Key nutrients to plan: B12, iodine, iron, vitamin D, calcium, omega-3 and protein.
- A 2019 review says well-planned vegan diets may be considered safe in pregnancy, with strong awareness of nutrients.
- Involve a doctor or dietitian from before conception if possible.
