## The objection at full strength

A plant-based burger comes with a long ingredient list, added salt and saturated fat from coconut oil. A beef patty is just beef. Surely the vegan version is the more processed and therefore the worse choice.

## Response

The question is open and we should not pretend otherwise. A [2025 review in Frontiers in Nutrition](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2025.1641234) describes plant-based meat alternatives as concentrated sources of plant protein formulated to mimic meat, and notes that there is debate about their healthfulness and about whether the benefits seen with whole-food plant-based diets carry over to them. Reading the label is sensible.

What the data show about the people who eat them is more encouraging. In the [UK National Diet and Nutrition Survey](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cdnut.2026.109401), adults who ate plant-based meat ate more fruit, vegetables and pulses and less animal meat than non-consumers, with higher intakes of fibre, vitamins A, C, E, folate, calcium, magnesium and zinc. Intakes of B12, vitamin D, iron and iodine did not differ. This is a cross-sectional survey (101 consumers), so it cannot show the products themselves are healthy; it shows they sit within diets that are not worse.

Comparing products fairly means comparing a burger with a burger: the [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/cancer-carcinogenicity-of-the-consumption-of-red-meat-and-processed-meat) classes processed meats such as sausages and ham as carcinogenic to humans and red meat as probably carcinogenic. Neither is health food. If processing is the worry, beans, lentils, tofu and tempeh are the minimally processed option for vegans.

## In short

- Whether plant-based meats are healthy is debated; check labels for salt and saturated fat.
- UK consumers of plant-based meat had better overall diets, which doesn't prove the products are healthy.
- Compare like with like: processed meat is classed as carcinogenic by WHO.
