## The objection at full strength

A healthy diet should deliver what the body needs from food. Vegans are told to take B12, and maybe vitamin D, iodine, omega-3, calcium and more. If the diet only works with a pharmacy shelf behind it, then it is not a complete diet.

## Response

Only B12 is non-negotiable. The [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025) requires "reliable sources of vitamin B12, such as fortified foods or supplements", while for other nutrients the advice is to plan: [Dietitians Australia](https://member.dietitiansaustralia.org.au/Common/Uploaded%20files/DAA/Resource_Library/2020/VF_A_Guide_to_Vegan_Eating.pdf) says vegans might also need to supplement calcium, iron and omega-3, and asks people to check with a dietitian or doctor first. Many people meet their needs through fortified plant milks, iodised salt, calcium-set tofu, legumes, nuts and seeds.

Supplements are also not unique to vegans. In the [German study of 36 vegans and 36 omnivores](https://doi.org/10.3238/arztebl.2020.0575), nearly all the vegans and one-third of the omnivores had taken a supplement in the previous four weeks. Using one does not mean a diet has failed.

We would not oversell this. A diet that depends on one pill a day is less forgiving than one that does not, and it is reasonable for sceptics to point that out. But the cost is small compared with what is at stake for animals, and most people find it a minor habit. For anything beyond B12, get a blood test or advice before buying supplements.

## In short

- Only B12 is non-negotiable for vegans; other nutrients can often be covered by food choices and fortified foods.
- Supplement use is common on mixed diets too.
- Check with a dietitian or doctor before adding supplements.
