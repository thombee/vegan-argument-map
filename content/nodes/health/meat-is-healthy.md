## The objection at full strength

Meat is one of the most concentrated sources of protein, B12, iron, zinc and other nutrients, in forms the body absorbs well. Human diets across history have included it. Dietary guidelines still include lean meat. A food that supplies so much, and that so many people thrive on, is hard to call unhealthy.

## Response

Most of this is true. The [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/cancer-carcinogenicity-of-the-consumption-of-red-meat-and-processed-meat) says meat can be part of a healthy diet and provides important nutrients. The [Australian Dietary Guidelines](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/australian-dietary-guidelines-2013.pdf) agree that animal foods are highly bioavailable sources of iron, calcium and B12, and that vegans must take care to replace them. We would not dispute any of it, and we do not claim that meat will make you ill.

Three points keep that from settling the question. First, nutrients are not the same as the foods that contain them. The ones meat is best for can be obtained elsewhere: the [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025) says appropriately planned vegan diets are nutritionally adequate, with a reliable B12 source. Second, the same WHO page notes that many national guidelines recommend limiting processed meat and red meat because higher intakes are linked to a greater risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and some cancers; its cancer agency classifies processed meat as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1) and red meat as probably carcinogenic (Group 2A). Third, in the large UK [EPIC-Oxford](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l4897) cohort, vegetarians had a 22 percent lower rate of ischaemic heart disease than meat-eaters after adjustment for sociodemographic and lifestyle factors.

We are not saying vegan diets are always superior: EPIC-Oxford also found [higher rates of fractures in vegans and of stroke in vegetarians](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0029665121003748) (see [Vegans are deficient in iron, calcium…](/a/nutrient-deficiencies/)). The claim is smaller: meat is not necessary for health, and it carries some measured risks. The reasons to avoid it rest on what it takes to produce it.

## In short

- Meat does provide B12, iron and zinc: WHO says it can be part of a healthy diet.
- These nutrients are available to vegans via planning, fortification and supplements.
- Processed meat is classed as carcinogenic and red meat as probably so; neither is required for health.
