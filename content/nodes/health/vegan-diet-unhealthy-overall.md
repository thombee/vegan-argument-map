## The objection at full strength

If vegans have more fractures, lower iron and low B12 intakes, then whatever benefits the diet offers must be outweighed by its costs. The claim that vegan diets are protective is advocacy, and the data show a trade-off.

## Response

Neither "vegan diets are a miracle" nor "vegan diets are unhealthy" is what the evidence says. A [meta-analysis of observational studies](https://doi.org/10.1080/10408398.2016.1138447) found vegetarians had about 25% lower risk of ischaemic heart disease and 8% lower cancer incidence, and vegans 15% lower cancer incidence, though the vegan result came from a limited number of studies. A summary of EPIC-Oxford by [Key and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0029665121003748) concludes that vegans have a lower risk of diabetes, diverticular disease and cataracts and a higher risk of fractures, and that overall the health of plant-based eaters is "generally good, with advantages but also some risks". Vegetarians there also had a higher risk of stroke, mainly haemorrhagic.

The German Nutrition Society's [2024 review](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/gesund-und-gruen-148427/) reaches a similar balance, rating the certainty of many findings as low to very low. These are observational studies, which cannot prove cause, and we should not claim the diet cures or prevents disease beyond what the professional bodies say. The [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025) says appropriately planned vegan diets are healthful and nutritionally adequate and may provide health benefits.

The honest summary is: a well-planned vegan diet is a sound way to eat with some things to manage. The ethical case does not depend on the diet being better than the alternatives; it depends on it being adequate, which the major bodies agree it can be.

## In short

- Evidence shows lower rates of heart disease and some cancers but also higher fracture risk: a mixed picture.
- No dietetic body says the diet cures disease.
- The case for veganism rests on animals, and the diet only needs to be adequate.
