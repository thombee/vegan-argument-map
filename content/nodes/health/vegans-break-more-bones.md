## The objection at full strength

The largest UK cohort of vegetarians and vegans, EPIC-Oxford, found more fractures among vegans. Bone density is lower too. This is one of the few places where the vegan diet shows a worse outcome in good prospective data, and the nutrients involved, calcium, vitamin D and protein, are the ones vegans are told to watch.

## Response

This is a genuine finding and the honest response is to take it on board. A [review of the EPIC-Oxford study](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0029665121003748) reports that vegetarians and vegans have lower bone mineral density than meat-eaters, and that vegans in that cohort had a higher risk of fractures. Germany's nutrition society, in its [2024 position](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/gesund-und-gruen-148427/), also lists poorer bone health and higher fracture risk among possible downsides.

What we do not know is how much of this is preventable. The same review says the extent to which the risks may be mitigated by optimal food choices, fortification and supplementation is not yet known. Observational data like these also cannot show cause: vegans may differ from meat-eaters in other ways than diet.

Practically: make sure you have a reliable calcium source (calcium-set tofu, fortified plant milks, some greens, supplements if needed), vitamin D, adequate protein and enough total energy. Ask a GP or dietitian whether anything in your own situation calls for a closer look.

## In short

- Vegans had a higher fracture risk in EPIC-Oxford: a real finding.
- Whether better calcium, vitamin D and protein planning removes the gap is not yet known.
- Plan for calcium, vitamin D and protein, and talk to a dietitian or GP.
