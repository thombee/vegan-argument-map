## The objection at full strength

You admit it yourselves: meat has more absorbable iron and zinc, and B12 only comes from animal foods. If plants are worse at supplying these nutrients, then eating meat is the better choice nutritionally, full stop.

## Response

The premise is correct. The [Australian Dietary Guidelines](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/australian-dietary-guidelines-2013.pdf) say animal foods are highly bioavailable sources of iron, calcium and B12 and that care is needed to include alternatives, and the [British Dietetic Association](https://www.bda.uk.com/resource/vegetarian-vegan-plant-based-diet.html) notes that plant sources of iron and zinc are absorbed less efficiently. The [Bakaloudi review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2020.11.035) found vegans with low average intakes of zinc, B12, calcium and selenium.

A better source is not the same as a necessary one. Absorption is a matter of dose and combination: vitamin C with iron-rich meals helps, and zinc and iron can come from legumes, nuts, seeds and wholegrains. The same review found no link between vegan diets and iron deficiency in terms of intake. B12 is the exception, where the answer is a supplement or fortified food. The dietetic bodies that stress these gaps are the same ones that conclude a planned vegan diet is adequate.

If your needs are higher, for example in pregnancy or heavy training, an individual plan is worth the effort. But the result is a few things to manage, not a diet that cannot work.

## In short

- Animal foods are better-absorbed sources of iron, zinc, calcium and B12: true.
- Vegans offset this with planning, vitamin C, fortification and, for B12, supplements.
- Better source does not mean necessary source.
