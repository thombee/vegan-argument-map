## The objection at full strength

This is the strongest single argument people have, and it deserves a straight answer. Every major dietetic body says vegans must take B12 from fortified foods or supplements. A diet that only works with a pill, the argument goes, is not how humans are meant to eat, and if it were right, nature would have provided B12 in plants.

## Response

The factual part is correct and we should not blur it. The [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025), the [British Dietetic Association](https://www.bda.uk.com/resource/vegetarian-vegan-plant-based-diet.html) and the [Australian Dietary Guidelines](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/australian-dietary-guidelines-2013.pdf) all say vegans need a reliable source of B12, from fortified foods or a supplement. Anyone who goes vegan should do so.

What the argument leaves out is where B12 comes from. It is not made by animals. A [review in Experimental Biology and Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1177/1535370217746612) explains that B12 is synthesised only by certain bacteria and archaea, not by plants or animals, and that cattle and sheep get it from bacteria living in their stomachs. People who eat meat are getting B12 second-hand, made by bacteria. Livestock can run short as well: veterinary research on [cobalt deficiency in lambs](https://doi.org/10.1080/00480169.2004.36418) deals with cobalt/B12-responsive animals, and farmed animals in some regions are supplemented.

So the real question is not whether the source is "natural". Whether a nutrient arrives in a pill, in fortified food or via a cow's gut bacteria does not decide whether eating animals is justified. Modern diets also depend on fortification and supplementation in many other ways. What matters is that the supplement is cheap, reliable and effective. For the "natural" and evolutionary side, see [It's natural; humans have always eaten meat](/a/its-natural/).

## In short

- B12 is made by bacteria, not by animals or plants; meat is a second-hand source.
- Vegans must use fortified foods or supplements: that is true and every dietetic body says so.
- Needing a supplement says nothing about whether it is right to eat animals.
