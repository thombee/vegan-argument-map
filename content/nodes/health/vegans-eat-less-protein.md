## The objection at full strength

A systematic review of European studies found that vegan diets are lower in protein than every other diet group. If vegans are eating less, the argument runs, then the evidence is that the diet does not deliver.

## Response

That finding is accurate: the [systematic review by Bakaloudi and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2020.11.035) found vegan diets lower in protein than other diets, and also flagged low intakes of B12, vitamin D, iodine, zinc, calcium and selenium. We say so openly because it is useful: it shows where to pay attention.

Two things follow. First, an average being lower does not mean people are below what they need. The same review notes that low intakes are not always associated with health impairments. Second, protein is among the easier nutrients to fix. The [Mariotti and Gardner review](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu11112661) found legumes, nuts and seeds enough to reach full protein adequacy in adults, so including them at most meals is the practical answer. Tofu, tempeh, lentils, chickpeas, peanut butter and soy milk all help.

If your intake is a concern, for example if you eat a lot of refined foods or have high needs, check with a dietitian. And this is a question about how to eat well as a vegan, not about whether animals should be eaten.

## In short

- Vegans do eat less protein on average; that is a prompt to plan, not proof of deficiency.
- Beans, tofu, tempeh, nuts and seeds close the gap easily.
- A dietitian can check your own intake.
