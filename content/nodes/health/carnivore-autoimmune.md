## The objection at full strength

Autoimmune conditions flare and remit. When someone goes carnivore and a condition goes quiet, it is natural to say the diet worked. Some doctors use elimination diets, and carnivore is the ultimate one.

## Response

Elimination diets can be a reasonable approach, and we do not dismiss them. Where we part company is on proof. The [2026 scoping review](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18020348) could find only nine human studies of the carnivore diet, all small, short and uncontrolled, so it cannot say whether the diet caused any improvement or whether it is safe over years. The authors of the [2029-person survey](https://doi.org/10.1093/cdn/nzab133) also say the results may not generalise and that long-term effects need study.

Conditions that fluctuate naturally make cause hard to read, and so does changing everything at once. A clinician-supervised elimination diet reintroduces foods one by one, with the aim of identifying a specific trigger and widening the diet again. That is a better route to a lasting answer than a lifelong all-meat diet, and it is open to people of any diet. If you are managing a serious illness, please do not stop medication or treatment on the strength of anyone's diet claims, including ours.

## In short

- Autoimmune conditions fluctuate, so improvement on a diet does not prove the diet caused it.
- No controlled trials support carnivore for autoimmune disease.
- A supervised elimination diet can identify specific triggers.
