## The objection at full strength

A real diet should come from real food. Vegans need fortified plant milks, B12 pills and fortified cereals. That is manufactured nutrition, not a natural diet.

## Response

Dietetic bodies are open about this. The [British Dietetic Association](https://www.bda.uk.com/resource/vegetarian-vegan-plant-based-diet.html) says the only reliable sources of B12 for vegans are fortified foods and supplements, and [Dietitians Australia](https://member.dietitiansaustralia.org.au/Common/Uploaded%20files/DAA/Resource_Library/2020/VF_A_Guide_to_Vegan_Eating.pdf) lists fortified plant milks, fortified meat alternatives and yeast extracts as B12 food sources. If that is a concern for you, you should be given the honest version of the trade-off, and this is it.

But the idea that nutrients must arrive "naturally" does not hold up well when we look at how B12 is made. As covered at [If you need B12 supplements, it isn't natural](/a/b12-unnatural/), B12 is produced by bacteria and not animals, so "natural" is a poor test of whether a source is acceptable. What matters is that the source is reliable.

The rest of a vegan diet can be built from ordinary whole foods: legumes, grains, vegetables, fruit, nuts and seeds. The fortified foods and pills fill a small number of gaps.

## In short

- Vegans need fortified foods or B12 supplements: that is the dietetic advice.
- The rest of a healthy vegan diet can be whole plant foods.
- See the B12 page for why 'natural' doesn't settle it.
