## The objection at full strength

Protein is the question every vegan hears, and it is not silly. Protein is essential, animal foods are rich in it, and people who lift weights, are older, or are recovering from illness have real needs. The common view is that meat, eggs and dairy are the natural way to get enough, and that plants fall short on quantity, quality or both.

Some of that is borne out by the data. When researchers measure what vegans actually eat, they do tend to eat less protein than meat-eaters, so the worry is not invented.

## Response

Lower than other diets is not the same as too low. A [review of protein and amino acids in vegetarian diets](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu11112661) concluded that ordinary protein-rich plant foods such as legumes, nuts and seeds are enough for protein adequacy in adults, and that the idea of amino acid deficiency has been "substantially overstated". The same review found no specific protein concerns for children, because their energy needs are so high relative to their protein needs, and said that older adults are a more complex case. A [systematic review of 48 studies](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2020.11.035) confirms vegans eat less protein than other groups on average, which is a reason to build meals around beans, lentils, tofu, tempeh and similar foods rather than a reason to doubt the diet.

Official guidance agrees. The [Australian Dietary Guidelines](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/australian-dietary-guidelines-2013.pdf) describe legumes as a valuable, cost-efficient source of protein for people eating vegetarian or vegan meals, and the [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025) says appropriately planned vegan diets are nutritionally adequate. The word "planned" matters: a vegan diet of chips and biscuits would be short of protein, as would any diet built on them.

None of this is medical advice. If you have particular needs, such as high training loads, pregnancy, old age or a medical condition, a dietitian can check your intake. Whether it is possible to get enough protein without animals is a nutrition question with a clear answer: it is. Whether animals should be used for food is a separate, moral question.

## In short

- Beans, lentils, tofu, tempeh, nuts, seeds and grains supply enough protein for adults when eaten in reasonable amounts.
- Vegans do tend to eat less protein than meat-eaters; that is a reason to plan meals, not evidence of deficiency.
- Dietetic bodies say appropriately planned vegan diets are adequate.
