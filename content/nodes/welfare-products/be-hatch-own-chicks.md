## The objection at full strength

I have a few hens and a rooster and I let them raise their own chicks. There is no hatchery involved, nobody is gassed or macerated, and the flock sustains itself. This is how chickens lived for centuries.

## Response

This is a different situation from the egg industry, and it avoids the specific problem the industry has. Hens raising their own chicks is closer to what chickens do.

The practical difficulty is that males hatch too. Many home flocks cannot keep many roosters, and keepers who do not want extras must find homes for them or kill them. The industry kills male chicks for exactly the reason a home flock struggles with them: [they cannot lay eggs and are not suited to meat production](https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/what-happens-with-male-chicks-in-the-egg-industry/). The home keeper faces a smaller version of the same decision.

If the answer is to keep every bird until it dies naturally, the flock is a sanctuary rather than a production system, and that is a stance any vegan can support. If the answer is to kill some of the birds, the moral question returns in a small form.

## In short

- Avoiding hatcheries avoids the industry's mass cull.
- Male chicks still hatch, which leaves the keeper with a choice.
- Keeping every bird for life is a sanctuary; culling the surplus is the same question at smaller scale.
