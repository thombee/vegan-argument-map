## The objection at full strength

Farmers do not take calves away to be cruel. A newborn calf is vulnerable to disease, and keeping it with its mother can spread infection. A calf raised in a clean pen and fed controlled milk is healthier. It is a management decision for the benefit of the calf, not a punishment for the cow.

## Response

Disease risk is a real reason, and the RSPCA does list it. It says most calves are removed within a day, [mainly to lower the risk of disease passing to the calf](https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/what-happens-to-bobby-calves/). A good farmer takes that seriously.

But two other findings are hard to square with a purely "for the calf" reading. In a controlled welfare study of 24 Holstein pairs, [both cows and calves reacted more strongly to separation at two weeks than at one day](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11179551/), which fits the idea that the bond strengthens quickly. In the same study, the calves left with their mothers longer gained weight at more than three times the rate over the first fortnight. The cost to the farmer was that those cows gave less milk at milking over those two weeks, because the calf was drinking some of it.

That is the honest summary. Early separation reduces some disease risk and keeps more milk for sale. It also means the cow and calf are parted at the point when the separation is easiest to arrange, not necessarily easiest for them. Because the whole system depends on cows calving, the calf and the milk cannot be separated from the question.

## In short

- Disease prevention is a real reason for early separation.
- A controlled study found cows and calves reacted more strongly to separation at two weeks than at one day.
- Calves kept with their mothers grew faster, but the cow's milk went to the calf.
