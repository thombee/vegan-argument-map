## The objection at full strength

The calf issue aside, the cow herself is not slaughtered early. She is milked through a long working life and sold when she is spent, and by then she has lived a full cow life. That is retirement, not killing.

## Response

The data do not describe retirement. A CSIRO-published analysis of about 2.45 million Australian culling records over 21 years found that the average age at culling was [about 6.75 years for Holstein cows](https://doi.org/10.1071/AN20195). The leading recorded reasons were infertility (17.0%), mastitis (12.9%) and low production (9.3%). "Old age" accounted for only 6.2% of records. The normal life expectancy of a dairy cow is [about 20 years](https://extension.psu.edu/have-your-cows-repaid-their-debts), which Penn State Extension says is very rarely observed on modern dairy operations.

So the usual pattern is that the cow is removed from the herd when her body can no longer meet the demands of repeated calving and heavy milk production. That is a decision made because she stopped being profitable, not because she was ready to die.

To be fair to the objection, some herds are managed for longevity, and a rise in herd life is something the industry itself says is desirable. A cow that reaches ten years is better off than one culled at four. But the endpoint is still slaughter at a fraction of her natural lifespan, and it does not change the calf problem.

## In short

- The average Australian dairy cow is culled at about 6.75 years.
- The leading reasons are infertility, mastitis and low production, not old age.
- Natural life expectancy is around 20 years.
