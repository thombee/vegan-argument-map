## The objection at full strength

Hobby beekeepers in particular spend a lot of time and money keeping colonies alive. They treat disease, give shelter and often plant for pollinators. Buying local honey supports that work, and the bees are better off than they would be left alone.

## Response

Many beekeepers are conscientious and fond of their bees. There is no reason to doubt it, and the respect they show the animals is real.

Two things are still true. First, the care and the honey are separate. A person can keep bees in a hive and not take the honey; the bees do not need to be harvested to be protected. Second, the honey is the colony's food store. Harvested honey is commonly replaced by sugar, and a 2023 study notes that [sucrose supplementation is safe and does not cause winter mortality](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani13030323). That is a reassuring detail for the bees, but it is also a reminder that the food was swapped for something cheaper so that we could keep what they made.

The environmental part of the argument is about whether keeping managed honeybees helps pollinators in general. That is a separate empirical question from whether taking honey is justified, and it does not depend on selling honey.

## In short

- Many beekeepers care about their bees.
- Caring for bees does not require taking the honey.
- Harvested honey is typically replaced with sugar for the colony.
