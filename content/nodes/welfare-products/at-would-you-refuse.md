## The objection at full strength

Imagine your child is sick and the only effective treatment was developed on animals. You would give it. So at the point that it matters, you value a human life above any animal's, and the whole argument falls apart.

## Response

I would give it, and so would nearly every vegan. The position was never that no human interest can outweigh any animal interest. It is that trivial human interests, such as taste, convenience or fashion, cannot justify killing.

This is a real distinction and we use it elsewhere. Most people think it can be right to harm one party to prevent a much greater harm to another, and also wrong to do the same thing for fun. The first is a case of necessity; the second is not. A medicine for a sick child is necessity. A burger is not.

It is worth noting that the thought experiment does not decide how animals should be treated in research generally. People can agree that in an emergency a human life comes first and still insist on replacing animal experiments wherever possible, and on not using animals for cosmetics, food or entertainment. The question of how much human interest justifies how much animal harm is a hard one, and the answer for a lifesaving drug is not the same as for lunch.

For the question of whether humans simply matter more, see [Humans are more intelligent, so we matter more](/a/humans-are-superior/).

## In short

- The vegan position is not that no human interest can ever outweigh an animal's.
- Necessity (a life-saving drug) differs from preference (taste, fashion).
- Accepting unavoidable medical use does not justify using animals where we have a choice.
