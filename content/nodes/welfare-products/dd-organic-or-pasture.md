## The objection at full strength

You are describing the big industrial dairies. My milk comes from a small organic herd on pasture, where the calves are looked after properly and the cows are well cared for. That is not the system you are describing.

## Response

Conditions do differ, and a pasture-based small herd is better for the cow than a large indoor operation. If you are going to buy milk, that is the better way to do it.

The difference is in degree, not structure. Whatever the farm, a cow has to calve to give milk, and a herd produces more calves than it can keep. RSPCA Australia says [all male calves and about a quarter of female calves are surplus to the herd](https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/what-happens-to-bobby-calves/). A small farm may raise those calves for beef, which is a better outcome than early slaughter (see [He raises the bull calves for beef](/a/sf-bull-calves-for-beef/)), but they still end up killed. The cows are still culled when their milk drops.

The wider objection to "humane" products is covered at [I only buy humane / free-range / local](/a/humane-meat/).

## In short

- Small pasture herds are better for the cow than industrial dairies.
- Any milking herd produces surplus calves and culls cows.
