## The objection at full strength

Nobody has to kill a cow to milk her. Milking is routine and many cows seem to tolerate it well. The cow is not harmed by giving milk, unlike meat, so dairy sits in a completely different category. Even many vegetarians draw the line here.

## Response

It is true that a milking cow does not die in the act of being milked, and it is also true that dairy involves less immediate killing than meat. The harm is not in the milking itself. It is in how milk is produced.

A cow produces milk after giving birth, so a dairy herd has to keep having calves. Most of them are not needed. RSPCA Australia explains that [all male calves and roughly a quarter of female calves are surplus to the herd](https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/what-happens-to-bobby-calves/), and at least 400,000 "bobby calves" are sent to slaughter each year, when they are only days or weeks old. The same source says most calves are taken from their mothers within a day of birth.

The cow then is not allowed to live out her life. An analysis of about 2.45 million Australian culling records found the [average age at culling was about 6.75 years](https://doi.org/10.1071/AN20195), with infertility, mastitis and low production leading the reasons. A dairy cow's normal life expectancy is about 20 years, an age [very rarely seen on modern dairy farms](https://extension.psu.edu/have-your-cows-repaid-their-debts).

So the accurate picture is that dairy doesn't kill the cow on the day she is milked. It kills her calves, and it ends her own life once she is less productive. Most of the people who feel uneasy about veal, and then drink milk, are drinking from the same system.

## In short

- Milk requires repeated calving, and the surplus calves are mostly sent to slaughter.
- Calves are usually removed from their mothers within a day.
- Dairy cows are typically culled at around 6-7 years, a fraction of a natural lifespan.
- The harm is built into producing milk, not just meat.
