## The objection at full strength

The headline studies were run by researchers with a stake in the answer. They asked owners who chose vegan diets whether their cats seem healthy, and owners are not vets. People who feed a vegan diet tend to want it to work. You cannot rely on that to put an obligate carnivore at risk.

## Response

These are fair criticisms and the authors themselves make most of them. The 2023 cat study lists [self-reported data, internet recruitment (about 91% of respondents were female and 70% in the UK), only 127 vegan cats, unverified diet quality, and funding by ProVeg International](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499249/). The earlier North American survey says its results are [subject to bias and methodological limitations](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-021-02754-8) and calls for further research.

What follows from that? Not that vegan cat food is dangerous, because the studies found no sign that it was, and not that it is safe, because they cannot show that. It follows that anyone who tries it should use a complete commercial formulation, involve a vet, and watch for signs of trouble. If you are unwilling to take that risk, feeding a meat-based diet is a defensible choice for an animal that has no say in the matter.

None of this is a case against veganism. The reason to be vegan is about the animals we do not need to eat, and the cat's dietary needs are a separate constraint, handled with care.

## In short

- The evidence on vegan cat diets is mostly owner-reported, and one large study was funded by a vegan organisation.
- That supports caution and veterinary advice, not confidence either way.
- A cat's diet is a separate question from the reasons for being vegan.
