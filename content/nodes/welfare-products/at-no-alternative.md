## The objection at full strength

You can't test the safety of a new drug on a cell culture. A whole body has organs, metabolism and immune responses that no dish can reproduce. Until there is something that does the same job, animal tests are the only responsible way to protect people in the first human trials.

## Response

In many areas that is true today. The law does not claim that animals have been replaced, and the Act that I mention keeps animal tests as an option. The US FDA Modernization Act 2.0 defines [nonclinical tests as including animal tests or non-animal, human-biology-based methods such as cell-based assays](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-117s5002es/html/BILLS-117s5002es.htm). It permits both, rather than abolishing either.

What has changed is the principle. Australia's code treats [replacement as the first of the 3Rs](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/about-us/publications/australian-code-care-and-use-animals-scientific-purposes): methods that achieve a purpose without using animals. A regulator that writes this into law expects alternatives to be developed and used when they work.

The claim that animal testing reliably protects people is also not the whole story. A 2022 review states that [90% of clinical drug development fails](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apsb.2022.02.002). That paper does not blame animal models, and its authors point to other causes, so it is not evidence that they are useless. It does show that passing earlier tests is a long way from being a safe, effective medicine, which is a reason to keep funding better methods.

The reasonable position is not "stop all testing tomorrow". It is to fund replacement, to require it wherever it works, and to refuse animal use for products where there is no medical need.

## In short

- Animal testing is still used and the law still allows it.
- Regulators in Australia and the US now explicitly provide for non-animal methods.
- A 90% failure rate in clinical development shows that earlier tests are not a guarantee.
- The case for replacement does not depend on a ban tomorrow.
