## The objection at full strength

I have thought about this. I do not buy factory-farmed products. I buy free-range eggs, pasture-raised meat and milk from farms where I can see the animals have space, sunlight and a normal social life. Those animals are not in a cage or a crate. They have a good life and a quick death, which is more than wild animals get. Surely there is a moral difference between that and the worst of the industry, and surely buying this way is the sensible middle path.

## Response

There is a real difference, and it is worth saying so plainly. An animal on pasture with room to move is better off than one in a cage or a crowded shed. If you are choosing between the two, choose the better farm.

The question is whether "better" is the same as "justified". Three things stay the same on the best farms.

**The animal is still killed young.** Meat chickens are typically slaughtered at four to six weeks old, and RSPCA Australia says their lifecycle of around 35 to 47 days compares with a natural lifespan of [up to 10 years](https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/how-are-meat-chickens-farmed-in-australia). Australian dairy cows are culled at an average age of about [6.75 years](https://doi.org/10.1071/AN20195). The farm is kind in the middle. The ending is the same.

**Labels promise less than they sound.** In Australia, "free range" on an egg carton legally means a [stocking density of up to 10,000 hens per hectare](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/food-labelling/understanding-claims/free-range) with regular outdoor access. Many farms do better than that, but the label alone will not tell you which.

**Some harms sit upstream of the farm.** Laying breeds are bred for eggs, so male chicks have no commercial use. RSPCA Australia says [the vast majority are killed on the day they hatch](https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/what-happens-with-male-chicks-in-the-egg-industry/), whatever system the hens are later kept in.

The deeper point is that none of this changes what is being done. If you would not accept it for a dog or a cat, a kind life followed by an early, unneeded death is hard to defend for a pig or a hen, who also want to keep living. Buying humane is a step in the right direction. The consistent endpoint is not buying the animal at all.

## In short

- Higher-welfare farms are better than factory farms. That is true and worth acknowledging.
- The animals are still killed far short of their natural lifespan, and male chicks and surplus calves are produced on every kind of farm.
- A "free range" label only guarantees a legal minimum, not the farm you picture.
- If an unnecessary death is wrong for the animals we love, a kind life beforehand does not make it right.
