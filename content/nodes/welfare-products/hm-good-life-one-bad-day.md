## The objection at full strength

A farm animal that lives well for months or years and then dies quickly has had more good days than bad. Without farming it would not have lived at all. Weighed up honestly, the good life outweighs one stressful day, so the whole arrangement leaves the animal better off.

## Response

The argument is weighing the wrong thing. Death is not a single bad day to be set against good ones. It ends every good day the animal would still have had. A hen slaughtered at five weeks, or a cow at six or seven, loses the future that she was entirely capable of valuing.

Notice also that we do not apply this reasoning anywhere else. If a family kept a dog happy and well fed for three years and then killed it painlessly for a stew, no amount of earlier happiness would make that acceptable. The usual response is that dogs are different, but the difference is attachment, not anything the animal feels. A pig can be as aware and as attached to life as the dog.

The "otherwise it would not have existed" point is its own objection, and it is answered at [farm animals wouldn't exist without us](/a/wouldnt-exist-otherwise/).

It is also worth conceding what is fair. The weighing exercise is a reasonable way to compare two farms. A good life is better than a bad one. It just cannot do the job of justifying the killing.

## In short

- Killing is not one bad day; it removes all the good days that were left.
- We would not accept "happy life, painless end" as a defence for killing a dog or a person.
- Whether the animal would have existed at all is a separate argument, answered elsewhere.
