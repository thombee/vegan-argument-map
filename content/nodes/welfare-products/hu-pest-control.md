## The objection at full strength

Deer, pigs and other feral animals cause real damage in Australia. If nobody controls them, ecosystems and farmland suffer. Recreational hunters are volunteers who do this at no cost to the public, and they provide welfare-minded control.

## Response

Invasive animal management is a real problem, and it has nothing to do with veganism one way or the other. Where population control is needed, the question is how to do it with the least suffering, and that is a good question to put to hunters and professionals alike.

The welfare outcomes are the measure. A study of professional shooters found that [85% of deer shot at were killed, with non-fatal wounding in 3.5% of those shot at and hit](https://doi.org/10.1071/WR21131), and the authors concluded that better shooter training would improve outcomes. The same logic applies to volunteers: control should be held to measurable welfare standards, not just to the intent.

It is also a separate argument from "hunting is more ethical than farming". Pest control may be justified without being a reason to hunt for pleasure or to prefer game to plant food. A hunter who controls feral animals because it is needed is not in the same moral position as one who hunts for sport or flavour.

## In short

- Controlling invasive animals is a real issue with its own welfare standards.
- Measured outcomes, including wounding rates, are what matter.
- It does not justify hunting as a food choice or a hobby.
