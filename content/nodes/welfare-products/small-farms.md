## The objection at full strength

I know the farmer. I have seen the animals in the paddock, I know their names and he knows them too. He is not a faceless corporation. He gets up at night for calving, he has never been cruel to one of them, and he would be offended to be lumped in with factory farms. Surely supporting a good local farmer is the opposite of what you are campaigning against.

## Response

Good farmers exist, and their animals usually do have better lives than animals in intensive systems. That is a real difference and the objection is right to insist on it. Nothing here requires believing farmers are cruel people.

The trouble is not the person; it is what the business has to do to survive. Three things are true of almost any animal farm, however well run.

**Animals are killed when they stop being profitable, which is early.** A meat chicken is slaughtered at four to six weeks old, when the natural lifespan is [up to 10 years](https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/how-are-meat-chickens-farmed-in-australia). The same logic applies to lambs, pigs and calves.

**Breeding produces animals the farm cannot use.** A dairy farm needs cows to calve. RSPCA Australia explains that [all male calves and roughly a quarter of female calves are surplus to the dairy herd](https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/what-happens-to-bobby-calves/), and at least 400,000 bobby calves are sent to slaughter each year. A small farm may handle that better than most, but it cannot make the arithmetic disappear.

**Someone has to do the killing.** Even if your farmer is gentle, the animals are transported to an abattoir or killed on the property. The farm's good care is a promise about the middle of the animal's life, not about the end.

You can appreciate a good farmer and still think the thing he sells should not be bought. It is the same moral question as for any other use of animals: do we have a right to take a life that does not need to be taken? If the answer is no, then a decent farmer is a decent person doing something that the rest of us should stop paying for.

## In short

- Good farms are better for animals than bad ones, and a good farmer is not a villain.
- Animals are still slaughtered young, and surplus animals are produced by breeding.
- The moral question is about the use of animals, not the character of the person doing it.
