## The objection at full strength

The worst part of the industry is the truck and the abattoir. A farmer who dispatches his own animals on the property, in a place they know, with someone they trust, spares them the fear and noise. That is close to the best death an animal could have.

## Response

A calm death is better than a terrified one, and anyone who cares about animals should prefer it. That is a real point in the farmer's favour.

But it answers a different question. It is about how to kill, not whether to kill. A quiet end is still an end, to an animal who wanted to go on living and who was not sick or suffering. The reason to avoid the killing is not primarily that it is frightening. It is that the animal loses its life for a meal we do not need.

There is also a practical caveat. Quick, low-stress killing is a skill, and mistakes happen. When it goes wrong the animal suffers, and the person doing it is usually a farmer under time pressure rather than an observer. This is a reason for caution, not a claim that any farmer is careless.

The related argument, that a painless death is harmless, is answered at [Killing is fine if it's painless](/a/death-without-suffering/).

## In short

- Less fear at the end is better, and worth wanting.
- It does not answer whether the animal needed to die.
