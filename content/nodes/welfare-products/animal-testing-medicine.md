## The objection at full strength

Almost every medicine you have taken, including vaccines and antibiotics, was developed with the help of animal experiments. If you take them, you benefit from the same practice you condemn. A vegan who refuses leather but takes paracetamol is not being consistent, and in a real emergency would not refuse a drug that might save their life.

## Response

This is right in one important respect: nobody can avoid every product of animal testing, and no sensible vegan would refuse a medicine that they or their child needed. Veganism is usually defined as avoiding exploitation and cruelty as far as is possible and practicable. When a doctor prescribes a drug and there is no animal-free version, avoiding it is not practicable.

That standard distinguishes between a choice and a constraint. A person can choose not to buy a leather bag. They cannot choose to have been born into a medical system that was built partly on animal tests. Where an animal-free version of a product does exist, such as many over-the-counter and cosmetic products, the vegan choice is to use it.

And the practice itself is changing. Australia's national code requires researchers to apply the [3Rs: replacing animals with other methods, reducing the numbers used, and refining techniques](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/about-us/publications/australian-code-care-and-use-animals-scientific-purposes). In the US, the FDA Modernization Act 2.0 (S.5002, 2022) amended drug law to refer to [nonclinical tests, which may include animal tests or non-animal methods such as cell-based assays](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-117s5002es/html/BILLS-117s5002es.htm) instead of specifying animal tests.

The objection also proves less than it seems. Even if some medicines cannot be avoided, that gives no reason to eat, wear or use animals where there is no medical need.

## In short

- Almost nobody can avoid every product of past animal testing, and no vegan is asked to refuse a needed medicine.
- Veganism asks that we avoid exploitation where possible and practicable.
- Regulation is moving toward replacing animals where non-animal methods can do the job.
- Unavoidable medical use gives no reason to use animals for food or clothing.
