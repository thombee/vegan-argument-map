## The objection at full strength

Faux leather is made from petroleum-based coatings, it cracks and peels within a few years, and it ends up in landfill. A leather bag or boots can last decades and can be repaired. On environmental grounds, real leather beats the fake.

## Response

There is something right in this. A well-made leather item that lasts for decades has a long useful life, and a cheap synthetic that falls apart in two years does not. Durability is a good criterion for any purchase.

The conclusion does not follow from it. The standard is not "plastic or leather", it is whether you need to buy a new item at all, and what it is made from if you do. Options include buying second-hand, repairing what you have, and choosing well-made products made from non-animal materials. A cheap synthetic is a bad purchase for the same reason a cheap leather one is.

The comparison also leaves the animal out. If durability were the only criterion, it would still not settle whether it is right to use an animal's skin when there are other materials.

## In short

- Durable, repairable goods are a good idea whatever the material.
- The alternative to leather is not only cheap plastic.
- Durability does not answer the question of the animal.
