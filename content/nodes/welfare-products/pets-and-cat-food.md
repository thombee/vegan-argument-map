## The objection at full strength

Cats are obligate carnivores. A vegan who keeps a cat has to feed it animals, which is the very thing they say is wrong, or else risk the cat's health on an ideology. Owning any pet at all is itself a use of animals. The vegan position cannot be consistent on pets.

## Response

Start with what is true. Cats are obligate carnivores: they need nutrients, such as taurine, that are found in animal ingredients, and a poorly planned diet can cause harm. A UTS report on a recent study notes that [taurine deficiency can cause retinal degeneration and cardiac disease, and that the research cannot conclude a solely vegan diet is safe or beneficial for cats in the long term](https://www.uts.edu.au/news/health-science/it-really-safe-feed-your-cat-vegan-diet). Anyone who cares about their cat should not experiment casually.

The evidence on well-formulated vegan cat food is thin but not alarming. A survey of 1,026 cats found that [owners of cats on plant-based diets did not report more health problems](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-021-02754-8), and a larger 2023 survey found [no statistically significant difference on any of seven general illness indicators](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499249/). Both rely on owner reports, and the second was funded by a vegan advocacy organisation. They are reasons for further study, not proof that it is safe.

So the honest position is this. A vegan can keep a cat, and the most defensible choices are (1) a nutritionally complete diet chosen with a vet's advice, and (2) accepting that a cat's welfare comes first. Some vegans feed their cats meat-based food for that reason, and that is a tension worth admitting rather than denying.

On the broader charge, keeping a rescued pet is not the same as exploiting an animal. Veganism is about not using animals as means to our ends; giving a home to an animal that needs one is the opposite. The ethical problems come with breeding and selling animals, which is why adopting is the consistent approach.

## In short

- Cats are obligate carnivores and need expert care on any diet; taurine is essential.
- Early survey evidence on vegan cat diets is limited and self-reported.
- Some vegans choose meat-based food for their cats for welfare reasons, and that is a reasonable position.
- Adopting an animal in need is consistent with veganism; breeding and buying is the problem.
