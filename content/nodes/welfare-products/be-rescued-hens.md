## The objection at full strength

I took in hens that would otherwise have been killed when their production fell. They are going to lay anyway. Throwing the eggs away is worse than eating them, and not eating them would not help a single animal.

## Response

Taking in hens that would have been killed is an act of kindness, and no vegan argument is against it. If they lay, the eggs are not producing any new demand. This is the case where the objection is at its strongest and many people who are otherwise vegan make exactly this choice.

The reasons some people still choose not to eat the eggs are modest. Laying may be hard on a hen. The fractures [seen in a laying breed in a small pilot study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0255234) are one sign of that, and some keepers prefer to return the eggs to the hens. There is also a habit risk: if the eggs are always eaten, it is easy for the household to drift back into treating eggs as a normal product.

The principle that matters is that no animal is bred or killed for the eggs. Where that is true, reasonable people disagree about the details, and it is not worth the energy of a fight.

## In short

- Rescuing hens is good, and eating eggs from them creates no new demand.
- Laying breeds may pay a physical cost, which is why some keepers return the eggs to the hens.
- The aim is no animals bred or killed for eggs; the rest is a judgement call.
