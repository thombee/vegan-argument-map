## The objection at full strength

A well-trained hunter takes a shot only when they are sure. The animal never knows what happened and dies in an instant. That is better than any death in nature, where predators, starvation and disease are far worse, and better than a stressed journey to an abattoir.

## Response

When it works, it is a fast death, and a responsible hunter is trying to make that happen. The same study that gives the 93% outright-kill figure also identifies [what makes it more likely: a comfortable position, a rest for the gun, aiming at the chest, heavier bullets, unhurried shots and regular practice](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0109698). Hunters who follow that advice will do better than average, and I would not argue against it.

There are two limits. Even skilled hunters cannot get to 100%, and the share that is wounded can suffer for a long time. And the comparison to a worse death does not help the justification: that death is quick does not explain why an animal who was not going to die today should be killed at all. The comparison to disease and starvation in the wild is addressed at [Animals kill each other in the wild](/a/animals-eat-each-other/).

## In short

- A clean shot is a better death than many; a responsible hunter aims for it.
- Studies show a minority of shots wound, especially under poor conditions.
- A quick death still ends a life that did not need to end.
