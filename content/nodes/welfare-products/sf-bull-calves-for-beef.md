## The objection at full strength

On a farm that raises its male calves to be beef, the calves are not discarded at a few days old. They graze for a year or more, then become food. The farm uses the animal fully. That is better than the industry norm, and the RSPCA itself says as much.

## Response

It is better, and the RSPCA does encourage it. It suggests that consumers [ask whether dairy farms find ways to grow bobby calves out for veal or beef](https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/does-the-rspca-have-animal-welfare-standards-for-dairy-production/) rather than sending them to slaughter within days. A calf that lives on pasture for a year has had more life than one killed in its first week.

Three limits are worth noting. The calf is still being bred in order to be killed. Calves on most dairy farms are also [removed from their mothers within a day](https://kb.rspca.org.au/knowledge-base/what-happens-to-bobby-calves/), so even the "good" calf starts life without her. And "nothing is wasted" is true of the product, not of the animal: the animal's own life is the thing that has been used.

The moral question here is not about wastage. If the calf's life matters to the calf, then ending it when it reaches a profitable weight is the same act as ending it at a week old, only later.

## In short

- Growing out calves for beef is a real welfare improvement over killing them days after birth.
- The calf is still bred in order to be killed, and typically still separated from its mother.
- "No waste" describes the carcass, not what was done to the animal.
