## The objection at full strength

A wild deer lives its whole life free, eating what it evolved to eat, and then is shot once from a distance, without ever seeing a truck or a pen. Compared with a factory-farmed pig, that is a good life and a quick death. Hunters often eat what they shoot, and they know the animal and the land better than a supermarket shopper. If you are going to eat animals, hunting is surely the more honest way to do it.

## Response

If the choice is between eating farmed meat and eating game, the hunted animal has probably had the better life. The objection is right about that.

Three caveats follow.

**Not every shot kills.** The best field data are from the UK. In a study of 2,281 shots recorded by 102 stalkers, [96% of deer were hit, and 93% of those were killed outright](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0109698). The rest were wounded, and the study linked wounding to rushed shots, awkward positions, distance and obscured targets. In Australia, a study of professional shooters found that [85% of deer shot at were killed, and 3.5% of those shot at and hit escaped wounded](https://doi.org/10.1071/WR21131). A deer that was shot multiple times took a median of 289 seconds to lose consciousness. Professional marksmen did better than that, and skilled hunters can too, but even a small share of wounded animals is a real cost.

**Hunting for food is an optional choice for most hunters.** For someone with a supermarket nearby, the animal does not have to be killed. The question is the same one as for farming: do we have a right to end a healthy animal's life when we have other food?

**Pleasure is a different motive.** Where the point is the sport or the trophy rather than the food, the justification is weaker still.

A hunter who takes a clean shot and eats the animal has done less harm than most meat eaters. That is a claim about degree. The vegan position remains that the animal's life was not ours to take.

## In short

- A wild animal's life is generally better than a farmed one's.
- Not every shot kills: studies show a minority of shots wound.
- For people with other food, hunting is still an unnecessary killing.
- Where the aim is sport or trophy, the justification is weaker still.
