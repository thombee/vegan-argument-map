## The objection at full strength

You are lumping together bottom trawling and a single line from a small boat. Line-caught fish involve little bycatch, and certified fisheries are independently audited. Choosing those is how a consumer can reward good practice. That is not the same as industrial fishing.

## Response

The difference is genuine. Gilman and colleagues' analysis of discards found that [trawl fisheries, especially bottom otter trawls, warrant intensified efforts to reduce discards](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-71021-x). Gear choice matters, and a person who avoids the fisheries with the highest discard rates is reducing that particular harm.

The limit is that selective gear solves the bycatch problem, not the fish problem. The target fish are still hauled out of the water and killed, and a 2024 review concludes that the [welfare of wild-caught fishes is generally very poor during and after capture](https://doi.org/10.1017/awf.2024.7). A label that certifies the stock does not say anything about how the individual fish died.

If you are comfortable with the premise that fish can feel (and the evidence is that they can; see [Fish don't feel pain](/a/fish-dont-feel-pain/)), then certification tells you the fishery is well managed, not that it is harmless. It is a step in the right direction, in the same way that higher-welfare farming is (see [I only buy humane / free-range / local](/a/humane-meat/)).

## In short

- Line-caught and certified fish involve less bycatch and better management than trawled fish.
- Neither addresses the capture and death of the target fish.
- Welfare of wild-caught fish is generally very poor during and after capture.
