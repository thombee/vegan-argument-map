## The objection at full strength

Vegan staples like quinoa, avocados, coconut milk and tofu made from soy grown in Brazil travel thousands of kilometres. A local farmer's meat or eggs travel almost none. Shortening supply chains seems the obvious way to reduce food's footprint.

## Response

It sounds right, and it is a very common belief, but the data show that distance is a minor part of food's footprint.

[Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/food-choice-vs-eating-local) reports that for most foods, transport is less than 10% of emissions, and around 5% across the food system as a whole. Land use change and farm-stage emissions make up more than 80% of the footprint for most foods. Air freight, the most carbon-intensive form of transport, accounts for only 0.16% of food miles. A US household that sourced all of its food locally would cut its footprint by 5% at most.

The same article makes the comparison directly: "eating local beef or lamb has many times the carbon footprint of most other foods". A kilogram of beef emits about 60 kg of greenhouse gases, against about 1 kg for peas. No amount of shortened transport closes that gap.

Supporting local growers can be worth doing for other reasons. But if the question is climate, the most reliable lever is the choice of food, not its distance.

## In short

- Transport is about 5% of food emissions; most of the footprint arises on the farm and from land use.
- Air freight is 0.16% of food miles.
- Local beef still has many times the footprint of imported plant foods.
