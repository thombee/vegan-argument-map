## The objection at full strength

An avocado from Mexico or Peru has a long supply chain. A local farmer's lamb or cheese does not. If you care about the environment, shorter supply chains must win.

## Response

This is the food miles argument, and it is covered in full on the [food miles](/a/food-miles/) page. In brief, [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/food-choice-vs-eating-local) reports that transport is only about 5% of food emissions, that air freight accounts for just 0.16% of food miles, and that land use and farm-stage emissions make up more than 80% of the footprint for most foods. It says plainly that "eating local beef or lamb has many times the carbon footprint of most other foods".

That does not put any single crop beyond criticism. But the comparison that matters is the one with the alternative meal, and on emissions the local steak is far ahead of the imported avocado.

## In short

- Transport is about 5% of food emissions.
- Local beef and lamb have many times the footprint of most other foods.
- Any one crop can be criticised, but the fair comparison is with the meal it replaces.
