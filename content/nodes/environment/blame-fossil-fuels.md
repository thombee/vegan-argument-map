## The objection at full strength

Burning coal, oil and gas is the main driver of climate change. Focusing on what people eat is a distraction that lets energy companies off the hook and puts the burden on individuals. Fix the power grid and transport first; the food system is a rounding error next to that.

## Response

Fossil fuels are the largest source of emissions, and nothing here says otherwise. Fixing energy is essential. The claim that food is a rounding error is where the evidence disagrees.

[Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/environmental-impacts-of-food), drawing on Poore and Nemecek, reports that food systems are responsible for around a quarter (26%) of global greenhouse gas emissions. The [Food Climate Research Network](https://www.tabledebates.org/publication/grazed-and-confused) puts livestock alone at around 14.5%. These are not small numbers.

More importantly, [Clark and colleagues (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aba7357) modelled what happens if we deal with fossil fuels and ignore food. They found that even if fossil fuel emissions were halted immediately, current trends in global food systems would prevent the 1.5 degree target and, by the end of the century, threaten the 2 degree target. The 2 degree target could be reached with less ambitious food changes, but only if fossil fuel and other emissions are eliminated soon.

The two aims are not in competition. The grid, transport and the food system all need to change, and a person controls the third directly. Pointing out that systems matter more than individuals is a fair political point, and it is dealt with on the [one person can't make a difference](/a/one-person-no-difference/) page.

## In short

- Food systems are around 26% of global emissions; livestock alone about 14.5%.
- A 2020 Science study found food emissions alone would prevent 1.5 degrees even if fossil fuels stopped now.
- Energy and food both need to change; neither excuses the other.
