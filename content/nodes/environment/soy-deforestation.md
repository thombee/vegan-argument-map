## The objection at full strength

Vegans love tofu and soy milk, and soy is the crop most associated with Amazon destruction. Fields of soy in Brazil have replaced forest and savannah, and global demand keeps pushing the frontier. You cannot claim to care about the environment and eat a crop that is cutting down the lungs of the planet.

## Response

The worry is reasonable, and soy expansion has done real damage, especially in Brazil's Cerrado. But the numbers on who eats the soy point the other way.

According to [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/soy), more than three-quarters (77%) of the world's soy is fed to livestock, with poultry taking 37% and pigs about a fifth. About a fifth goes to direct human use, much of it as soybean oil, and the foods vegans associate with soy, tofu, soy milk, tempeh and edamame, are about 7% of global demand. Biofuels are around 4%.

On deforestation itself, the same source reports that beef drives 41% of tropical deforestation, and that in the Brazilian Amazon "it is pasture, not soybean production, that is driving most of the deforestation". Cattle ranching accounts for 72% of deforestation in Brazil. Soy expansion into forest in the Amazon fell sharply once the soy moratorium started: from 30% of expansion before the agreement to 1% by 2014.

So a person who eats tofu is eating a sliver of the crop, while a person who eats chicken, pork or farmed salmon is, indirectly, eating a larger share of it. If your concern is the forest, the most effective step is to remove the animal products fed on soy and the beef grazed on cleared land. Buying soy foods made with certified or non-deforestation soy is a sensible refinement.

## In short

- 77% of soy is livestock feed; tofu, soy milk and similar foods are about 7%.
- In the Brazilian Amazon, pasture rather than soy drives most deforestation.
- Cutting animal products reduces demand for both feed soy and cleared pasture.
