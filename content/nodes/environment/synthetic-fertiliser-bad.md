## The objection at full strength

Synthetic fertiliser comes from fossil gas, leaks into rivers and emits nitrous oxide. Manure is a natural recycling loop. Surely a farm that uses its animals' manure is greener than one buying chemicals.

## Response

The pollution from synthetic fertiliser is real. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/environmental-impacts-of-food) reports that agriculture causes 78% of global ocean and freshwater eutrophication, and that applies to fertilisers of every kind.

On the idea that manure is the green alternative, the picture is mixed. The [fertiliser analysis](https://ourworldindata.org/how-many-people-does-synthetic-fertilizer-feed) notes that recycling nutrients means by definition a limited supply: manure can maintain nitrogen levels but cannot create more. It also says that organic farming can sometimes have a greater environmental impact than conventional agriculture, under some measures.

The goal that follows is to use nutrients more precisely, from whatever source, and to waste less. That is compatible with a plant-based food system, which [Our World in Data's land-use analysis](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets) estimates would need about a quarter of today's agricultural land. Less land and less feed crop to fertilise is a smaller problem to solve.

Where a farm already has manure, using it is better than wasting it. That is not the same as a case for keeping animals in order to produce it.

## In short

- Synthetic fertiliser pollutes, and so does over-applied manure.
- Manure supply is limited and exists because of the animals; it cannot feed the world alone.
- A plant-based system needs far less land, leaving less to fertilise.
