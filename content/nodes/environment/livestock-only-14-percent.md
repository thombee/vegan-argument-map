## The objection at full strength

Veganism is sold as a climate solution, but the number the FAO gives for livestock is 14.5%. That is less than a fifth of the total. Some activists quote a much higher figure. If vegans are using an inflated number, why trust the rest?

## Response

Quoting the right figure matters, and the 14.5% figure from the FAO, which the [FCRN report](https://www.tabledebates.org/publication/grazed-and-confused) retains as its reference, is the standard one. Still, 14.5% is a lot for one sector, and the report itself notes there is less agreement about the impact of different animal types and production systems. A fair summary is that livestock is a major source, not the only one, and the figure should be quoted as it is.

The emissions share also leaves out the land. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/environmental-impacts-of-food) reports that Poore and Nemecek found 43% of ice- and desert-free land is used for agriculture, with 83% of that used for animal-sourced foods. The carbon that land could store if allowed to regrow is not in the emissions accounts. [Hayek et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41893-020-00603-4) estimate that cost at 152.5 gigatons of carbon in living biomass.

So a fair reading is that animal farming is a major but not an overwhelming source of emissions, and a still larger use of land. That is enough to make diet one of the biggest choices an individual can make, without exaggeration.

## In short

- Quote 14.5% for livestock as the standard figure, without inflating it.
- It leaves out the land: 83% of agricultural land is used for animal-sourced foods.
- Both numbers together make diet a major climate lever, without exaggeration.
