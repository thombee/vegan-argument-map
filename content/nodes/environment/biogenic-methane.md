## The objection at full strength

The carbon in cow methane came from grass, which took it from the air. The methane then breaks down fairly quickly, and the grass grows back to take it up again. So unlike fossil carbon from underground, cattle are just cycling carbon that was already part of the system. And there were vast herds of wild ruminants before agriculture.

There is also a recent scientific argument that the usual 100-year accounting overstates the warming from a steady, unchanging herd, because methane is short-lived. On that view, cattle in stable numbers are close to climate neutral.

## Response

This objection contains real science, so it deserves a careful answer rather than a dismissal.

Methane is short-lived and does not accumulate like CO2. A constant flow of methane roughly holds its warming contribution steady; it does not keep adding to it. Our World in Data [notes](https://ourworldindata.org/carbon-footprint-food-methane) that methane decays fast, and that a gradual decline in methane emissions would stabilise the temperature attributed to it. The 100-year accounting method does have limits for short-lived gases, and honest researchers say so.

But "not adding more warming" is not "doing no harm". The warming is already in the system, and methane is a major part of it. Reporting on the UN's methane assessment says methane is responsible for about 30% of warming since pre-industrial times, that livestock account for roughly 32% of human-caused methane, and that a 45% cut in human-caused methane would prevent up to 0.3 degrees of warming by 2045 ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/agriculture/reduce-human-caused-methane-emissions-by-45-to-avoid-worst-of-climate-change-un-76837)). Cutting livestock methane is therefore one of the quickest ways to slow warming.

Also, methane is only part of the picture. Even with methane removed from the calculation entirely, [average beef still emits 36 kg CO2eq per kg](https://ourworldindata.org/carbon-footprint-food-methane), nearly four times chicken, from feed, land clearing and fertiliser.

The "natural cycle" framing is also about carbon, not suffering. Whether herds of farmed animals are climate neutral does not determine whether it is right to raise and kill them.

## In short

- Methane is short-lived, so steady emissions hold warming steady; they do not keep adding to it.
- Cutting livestock methane is one of the fastest ways to slow warming, which is why it is a priority.
- Even without methane, beef's footprint is far above plant foods.
