## The objection at full strength

You accept that average beef is bad, because average beef includes cleared rainforest and feedlots. But the footprint varies hugely between farms. If you buy only from a pasture-based farm with good practices, your beef is on the efficient end of the range, and you are being more responsible than someone who eats a vegetarian pizza made with air-freighted cheese.

## Response

The variation is real. Poore and Nemecek found that [impacts can vary 50-fold](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaq0216) among producers of the same product, and it does matter which beef you choose if you are going to eat beef.

But the range does not overlap with plants in the way the argument needs. Our World in Data's [analysis of the same dataset](https://ourworldindata.org/less-meat-or-sustainable-meat) puts the best 10% of beef producers at about 9 kg CO2eq per 100 g of protein. Average peas are 0.4, beans 0.65, nuts 0.8 and tofu 1.6. Even the worst 10% of plant producers sit at 0.8 for peas, 2.4 for nuts and 3.5 for tofu. The best beef is about two and a half times the worst tofu. The authors' summary is that plant protein sources still have a lower footprint than the lowest-impact meat.

One more caveat is that the low-emission end is a small slice of what is actually sold. The same page notes that production above 11 kg CO2eq per 100 g of protein supplies 25% of protein but causes 70% of its emissions. A shopper cannot reliably tell which end a given steak comes from, and a "grass-fed" label does not settle it.

Choosing the best producer is a sensible harm-reduction step if you will not change your diet. It is not the same as choosing a low-impact diet, and it leaves the moral question where it was.

## In short

- The best 10% of beef producers (about 9) still emit far more per 100 g of protein than the worst 10% of tofu (3.5) or peas (0.8).
- Producer variation is large, so choice within beef matters, but it does not close the gap with plants.
- Low-impact beef is a small slice of what is sold, and labels do not guarantee it.
