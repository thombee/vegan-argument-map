## The objection at full strength

This is one of the strongest objections. Plenty of the world's land is too steep, dry, cold or stony to plough. If you cannot grow wheat or lentils there, then sheep and cattle that eat the grass are turning a landscape that cannot feed us directly into food. Vegans, the objection goes, are ignoring that animals can use land plants cannot.

The data supports part of this. Our World in Data reports that [two-thirds of pastures are unsuitable for growing crops](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets), and a well-known analysis by Mottet and colleagues found that most of what livestock eat is not something humans currently eat.

## Response

The factual point is correct and I would not dispute it. Two-thirds of pasture is unsuitable for crops, and [Mottet et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gfs.2017.01.001) estimate that 86% of livestock feed is material people do not currently eat, that people do not eat. A rangeland sheep is not competing with a lentil field.

Three things still undercut the conclusion.

First, a third of pasture can grow crops, and the remaining land is not free. [Hayek et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41893-020-00603-4) estimate that pastures for ruminant meat and dairy account for 72% of the carbon animal farming costs by keeping native vegetation from regrowing. Land that cannot grow wheat can often grow forest, scrub or native grassland, which store carbon and hold wildlife.

Second, the argument defends a small part of the food system. Animal products take roughly 80% of agricultural land when pasture and feed crops are counted together, according to [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets). A large share of the world's cereals goes to animal feed, so the marginal-land case does not describe every meal.

Third, even at the best, the argument is about land and efficiency. It says nothing about whether a sentient animal should be killed to produce the meal. Where people have no alternative way of eating, that is a different conversation. In a supermarket, there is an alternative.

## In short

- About two-thirds of pasture is unsuitable for crops, and most livestock feed is not human-edible; both are true.
- That land is not free: it could regrow vegetation that stores carbon.
- A large share of the world's cereals also goes to animal feed.
