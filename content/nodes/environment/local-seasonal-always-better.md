## The objection at full strength

Fine, food miles matter less than I thought, but I still prefer local, in-season vegetables over things flown halfway round the world. Isn't that the best of both worlds?

## Response

Local seasonal produce is a good choice and I would not talk anyone out of it. It supports regional growers and tends to be fresher.

The caveat is that "local" is only better when it is also in season. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/food-choice-vs-eating-local) cites studies in which Spanish lettuce imported to the UK in winter produced three to eight times lower emissions than lettuce grown locally, and Swedish greenhouse tomatoes used about ten times the energy of tomatoes imported from southern Europe where they were in season. Growing out of season can cost more energy than shipping a crop that is in season elsewhere.

The bigger point is the order of priorities. Switching a beef meal to a legume meal does far more than switching an imported vegetable to a local one, which is where most of the difference lies. If you prefer local vegetables, enjoy them. Just do not use them to justify the steak.

## In short

- Local is better when it is also in season.
- Heated glasshouse produce can emit more than imports grown in season.
- The food choice (animal vs plant) matters far more than local vs imported.
