## The objection at full strength

Animals eat what we cannot or will not. Brewers' grain, oilseed cake, straw, food scraps. If livestock disappeared, those by-products would need a new use or end up in landfill. Raising animals on them is circular, efficient and arguably the most sustainable thing a farm can do.

## Response

Using waste well is a good principle, and Mottet and colleagues' [figure of 86% non-edible feed](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gfs.2017.01.001) shows that livestock do use plenty of it. A pig on food scraps is a very different thing from a pig on imported soy.

The same paper has the other half of the story. Producing 1 kg of boneless meat takes on average 2.8 kg of human-edible feed in ruminant systems and 3.2 kg in non-ruminant systems. In other words, the portion of feed that people could eat still exceeds the meat produced. And Our World in Data notes that [41% of the world's cereals go to animal feed](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets), against 48% eaten directly by people.

The supply of by-products is also set by the human food system, and does not grow because demand for meat does. Meeting extra demand takes extra grain, soy and land.

Finally, whether a cow or something else makes best use of the brewer's grain is an efficiency question. Whether the cow should be killed is not answered by it.

## In short

- Livestock really do eat a large amount of material people do not eat.
- Even so, ruminant systems use 2.8 kg of human-edible feed per kg of boneless meat.
- By-product supply is fixed; meat demand is not.
