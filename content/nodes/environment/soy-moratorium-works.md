## The objection at full strength

If soy-driven deforestation fell to 1% of expansion, the problem is solved. Then vegans have nothing to complain about on soy, and the objection should be dropped from both sides.

## Response

This is half right, and I would rather say so than overstate the case. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/soy) reports that after the Soy Moratorium, soy's share of expansion at the expense of forest fell from 30% in the two years before the agreement to 1% by 2014. That is a real achievement, and it is why the Amazon discussion now centres on cattle.

The caveat is that protection in one region does not stop clearing in another. The same source reports that in 2015 only 13% of soy expansion came from the Amazon, while 48% came from the Cerrado.

For this site's purposes, the point is not that soy is blameless. It is that the people who eat soy directly are a small share of the demand for it, and the pressure on the Cerrado is driven mostly by feed. Fixing the Amazon problem further needs less pasture, and fixing the Cerrado problem needs less feed soy. Both are reduced by eating fewer animal products.

## In short

- The moratorium cut soy-linked clearing in the Amazon from 30% to 1% of expansion.
- It does not cover the Cerrado, where nearly half of 2015 expansion occurred.
- Remaining pressure is mostly feed and pasture.
