## The objection at full strength

Healthy soil needs nutrients, and for thousands of years farmers have returned manure to the land. Without livestock, how do you close the loop? Vegan farming would have to rely on synthetic fertiliser, which has its own damage, or mining nutrients from the soil until it is exhausted.

## Response

Manure is valuable, and in a farm that has animals it is sensible to use it. The question is whether crops depend on animals. They do not.

Plants need nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium, and they do not mind where these came from. Legumes such as clover, beans and lucerne fix nitrogen from the air, and grown as a rotation or green manure they can supply it to the crops that follow. Stockless organic farming is a recognised system, and the study below notes such systems are becoming more common in Europe as livestock farms decline. A [three-year field experiment](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jsusta/v16y2024i5p2152-d1351495.html) on stockless cropping compared processed red clover (fermented or composted) with fresh clover and granulated cattle manure, and found the processed clover improved productivity and nitrogen use for at least two years.

I should be straightforward about the limits. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/how-many-people-does-synthetic-fertilizer-feed) notes that synthetic nitrogen supports about half of the world's population, that manure supply is inherently limited, and that legumes tend to be lower-yielding than cereals. Much of today's manure exists because we raise so many animals in the first place, partly on synthetic nitrogen. A fully stockless world would still use some synthetic fertiliser and would need careful nutrient management.

So manure is a convenient resource, not a requirement. It is a reason to design farming carefully, not a reason to keep farming animals.

## In short

- Crops need nutrients, not animals; legumes and green manures can supply nitrogen.
- Stockless farming exists and is studied, but is harder and can yield less.
- Synthetic fertiliser feeds about half the world, and a vegan food system would still use some.
