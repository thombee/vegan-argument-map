## The objection at full strength

If palm oil clears rainforest, the ethical answer is to refuse it. Vegans should lead by checking labels, and the industry should be forced to change by boycotts.

## Response

Avoiding deforestation-linked palm is a sound instinct, and checking labels is easy to do. The difficulty is what happens if you simply swap it for something else.

[Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/palm-oil) reports that palm oil needs about 0.3 hectares per tonne, against around 1.4 for sunflower and rapeseed, 3.7 for coconut and about 7 for groundnut. Replacing the world's palm oil would take more land overall, not less: the article concludes that a global boycott "would simply shift it elsewhere and at a greater scale".

So the more effective step is to demand that palm oil is grown without clearing forest, and to eat fewer processed foods that rely on it. The problem is where and how it is grown, and the efficient crop is not the wrong one in itself.

None of this changes the main point. Palm oil is used across a wide range of foods and household products, and it is not specific to vegan diets.

## In short

- Palm is the most land-efficient vegetable oil per tonne.
- A blanket boycott can shift production to crops needing more land.
- Target deforestation-linked palm, and eat less processed food.
