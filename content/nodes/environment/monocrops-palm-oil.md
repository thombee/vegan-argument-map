## The objection at full strength

Vegan products are full of palm oil, and endless fields of soy, wheat and maize are monocultures that wipe out biodiversity. Mixed farms with animals look more natural and varied than a landscape of one crop.

## Response

Monoculture damages biodiversity, and palm oil plantations have replaced rainforest in Indonesia and Malaysia. Those are real harms.

But two facts complicate the picture. First, most of the monoculture cropland is already feeding animals. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets) reports that 41% of the world's cereals go to animal feed, and the soy page shows [77% of soy goes to feed](https://ourworldindata.org/soy). Remove the animals and you need far less land: the same source puts the agricultural land needed on a plant-based diet at about 1 billion hectares, down from 4.1 billion.

Second, palm oil is not a vegan monopoly. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/palm-oil) reports that 68% of palm oil goes to food, 27% to industrial uses like soaps and cosmetics, and 5% to bioenergy, and that includes food for animals. It is also relatively efficient: palm yields about 0.3 hectares per tonne, against around 1.4 for sunflower and rapeseed. The same page concludes that "a global boycott of palm oil would not fix the problem; it would simply shift it elsewhere and at a greater scale."

That points to sensible steps: choose certified, deforestation-free palm oil or other oils, and eat less processed food. But the numbers do not make a vegan diet the monoculture villain.

## In short

- Most of the world's monoculture cropland grows feed for animals.
- A plant-based diet would need roughly a quarter of today's agricultural land.
- Palm oil is used across food, cosmetics and fuel, and boycotting it can shift harm elsewhere.
