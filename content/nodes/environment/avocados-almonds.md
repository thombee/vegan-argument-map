## The objection at full strength

Almonds are grown largely in drought-prone California, and avocados need a lot of water and travel long distances. It seems hypocritical for vegans to criticise beef while eating fruit and nuts with a big water footprint, and the almond milk in a latte is a symbol of that problem.

## Response

The criticism is fair about almonds and water. Almond milk is one of the thirstier plant milks. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/environmental-impact-milks) says as much: almond milk has lower emissions and uses less land than soy milk, but needs more water and causes more eutrophication. It also concludes that there is no clear winner on every metric.

The question is what the almond milk is being compared with. Using [data derived from Poore and Nemecek](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/environmental-footprint-milks), a litre of almond milk takes about 371 litres of water, against 628 litres for dairy milk and about 28 for soy milk. Dairy milk also produces about 3.15 kg of CO2eq per litre against 0.7 for almond milk, and uses about 9 square metres of land against half a square metre. Across all the plant milks, the article says cow's milk uses two to twenty times as much freshwater, around three times as much greenhouse gas and around ten times as much land.

Nuts as a whole have a low climate footprint too: the Poore and Nemecek [average for nuts](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/ghg-per-kg-poore) is about 0.43 kg CO2eq per kg, against about 99 for beef from beef herds.

The sensible response is to use less of the thirsty foods and more of the efficient ones. In the same dataset, oat milk takes about 48 litres of water per litre and soy milk about 28. Water scarcity is a real problem for almond regions, and it is reasonable to choose accordingly. It does not turn the animal-based option into the better one.

## In short

- Almond milk is one of the thirstiest plant milks, but uses less water than dairy milk (371 L vs 628 L per litre).
- Dairy uses about ten times the land and about three times the emissions of plant milks.
- Choosing oat or soy milk avoids the almond water problem entirely.
