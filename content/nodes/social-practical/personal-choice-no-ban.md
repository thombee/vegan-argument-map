## The objection at full strength

Maybe you can argue the point over dinner. But where this ends up is laws: bans, taxes, restrictions. I don't want a state that polices my plate, and I think people have the right to eat what they like.

## Response

There is a real distinction here, and it is worth keeping. Whether something is morally wrong and whether it should be banned are different questions. Plenty of things we think are unkind are legal, and a good deal of what vegans do day to day is simply choosing differently and talking about why.

Policy questions do exist. Governments already regulate how animals are kept and killed, and they fund and subsidise parts of farming, so "the state staying out of it" is not what the current system looks like. People disagree, reasonably, about which reforms are wise or fair, whether that is subsidy changes, labelling or welfare rules.

But a worry about future laws is not an argument about the ethics of the present choice. If animals matter, the moral question remains whether or not any law ever follows. And you can hold the view that people should be free to choose while still being open to hearing why you might choose differently. See also [it's legal, so it's fine](/a/its-legal/).

## In short

- Wrong and illegal are different questions.
- Government is already involved in how animals are farmed.
- Worry about future laws does not answer the ethical question.
