## The objection at full strength

Veganism looks like a rich Western movement telling people elsewhere, including Indigenous and minority communities, how to eat. There is a history of outsiders policing the food of people they know little about. I'd rather defer to people who live inside those cultures.

## Response

The worry about outsiders policing other people's food is legitimate. Nobody should lecture a community about its own foodways, and the question of subsistence peoples has its own page: see [what about Inuit and other Indigenous peoples?](/a/indigenous-peoples/).

But the premise that veganism is a Western import doesn't hold up. Plant-based eating has deep roots in several traditions. According to [Pew's survey of religion in India](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/06/29/religion-in-india-tolerance-and-segregation/), 44% of Hindus and 92% of Jains say they are vegetarian. Concern for animals is not a Western monopoly.

And an argument doesn't belong to a country. If the case is sound, it applies to anyone; if it is weak, then the nationality of the person making it won't rescue it. The most useful response to a Western vegan who is condescending is to criticise the condescension, and then look at the reasons themselves.

## In short

- Don't lecture communities about their own foodways.
- Plant-based traditions exist in many cultures.
- An argument stands or falls on its reasons.
