## The objection at full strength

Humans are made in God's image and animals aren't. The tradition puts human life at the centre. Treating a pig like a person is a category mistake and, in a world with so much human suffering, a distraction.

## Response

Taking human dignity seriously doesn't conflict with this view. The argument isn't that a pig is a person, or that human needs rank equally with animal ones. It is that being at the centre brings responsibility, not a free hand.

That is how the Genesis passage reads to many: dominion paired with [tilling and keeping](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+1%3A26-31%3B+Genesis+2%3A15&version=NRSV). Pope Francis, in [Laudato Si'](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html), rejects a "tyrannical anthropocentrism unconcerned for other creatures" and affirms that other creatures have a value of their own. Human-first and animal-conscious can sit together.

On the point about distraction: concern for animals does not use up a fixed supply of concern for people, and most people who care about one care about the other. If there are suffering humans needing help, that is a reason to help them too, not a reason to ignore what we do to animals.

## In short

- Human dignity brings duties, not a free hand.
- Laudato Si' rejects 'tyrannical anthropocentrism'.
- Caring for animals doesn't compete with caring for people.
