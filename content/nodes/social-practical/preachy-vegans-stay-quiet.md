## The objection at full strength

If advocacy puts people off, the sensible thing is to live your life, eat your own food and say nothing. People come round when they're ready. Nobody likes being told what to do.

## Response

There is something to this. Quiet example does persuade, and many people change because a friend cooked them something good, not because someone argued with them. Vegans should not corner people at meals.

But "say nothing" cannot be a general rule. Every moral movement that has succeeded relied on people speaking up, often unwelcomely: against slavery, for children's protection, for workplace safety. If someone sincerely believes billions of animals are being harmed, staying silent forever is not neutral, because the harm continues. The same people who say "keep your views to yourself" would not want that rule applied to causes they care about.

The reasonable middle is the one most people already use: speak when asked or when the setting fits, be honest without being aggressive, and accept a "no thanks" gracefully. Vegans also benefit from being open about the real difficulties and their own imperfections, which tends to make a conversation easier on both sides.

## In short

- Quiet example works, but it isn't the only legitimate approach.
- Every successful moral movement involved speaking up.
- Respectful, invited conversation is the middle path.
