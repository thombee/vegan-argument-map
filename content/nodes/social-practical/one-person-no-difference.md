## The objection at full strength

There are billions of people and tens of billions of animals killed every year. One person skipping chicken won't be noticed by a supply chain that moves in the millions. Real change comes from governments and big companies. My leftover dinner isn't going to save anything.

## Response

It is true that no single purchase visibly changes anything. A shop does not notice one missing chicken, and a farm's output does not shift because of one household. If you think of impact as something you could see, one person's effect looks like nothing.

But that logic proves too much. Elections, recycling, and the sums of small gifts that fund huge efforts all depend on individuals who each look negligible. Demand is the sum of individual choices, and supply responds to totals. When many people change, retailers stock differently and producers adjust. The total is made up of people who each thought they were just one.

The scale also matters. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/land-animals-slaughtered-for-meat) tracks FAO figures for land animals slaughtered for meat each year, with a related analysis putting it at more than 80 billion, and that count excludes deaths in egg and dairy production. If each person is responsible for a share of that, then the share is not zero.

And there is a second reason not tied to outcomes at all: whether you take part. Even if your abstention changed nothing, buying an animal product is still a decision to be a customer of the practice. Most of us think that matters for things like fraud or cruelty, even when we cannot stop it alone.

## In short

- Demand is made up of individual choices.
- Change in supply responds to totals, not to any one purchase.
- Not participating is a reason in itself.
