## The objection at full strength

The food isn't the hard bit. It's the dinner at my in-laws, the work function, the road trip through a town with one pub. I don't want to be the guest who makes everyone fuss, and I don't want to turn every meal into a negotiation.

## Response

This is probably the most honest version of the objection, because it is about people, not recipes. Nobody wants to be difficult, and food is how families express care.

What helps in practice is mostly planning and tone. Look at menus ahead of time; many places can adapt a dish, and a chain restaurant or a curry house often has something. Tell hosts early and offer to bring a dish to share, so they are not scrambling. Treat a mistake as an accident rather than an issue. Hosts generally appreciate notice.

If you do end up somewhere with nothing suitable, eating what is there once is an imperfect but human outcome. Veganism is defined as avoiding exploitation "[as far as is possible and practicable](https://www.vegansociety.com/go-vegan/definition-veganism)", and a hard evening in a rural town is the kind of case it allows for. It is the regular choices, week in and week out, that matter most.

## In short

- Social eating is the hardest part for most people.
- Plan ahead, tell hosts early, bring a dish.
- Occasional impracticality is expected; the pattern matters.
