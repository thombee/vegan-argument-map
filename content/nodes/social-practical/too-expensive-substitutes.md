## The objection at full strength

That's all well and good for lentils, but people want burgers, nuggets and cheese. When I look at the shelf, the plant-based version is nearly always dearer. If I'm going to give up something, I want a swap that doesn't bankrupt me.

## Response

Often true, and nobody should pretend otherwise. Products made with new processes and smaller production runs tend to cost more, and some substitutes are a treat rather than a daily item.

The Oxford study cited above is explicit that its cost savings came from whole foods and left out highly processed meat replacements. So the finding is that you can eat well and cheaply without them, not that every vegan product is cheap.

In practice, a lot of people use a mix: staples for most meals and the occasional pricier product. Familiar dishes such as chilli, curry, pasta with a bean sauce or a stir-fry with tofu are cheap to make and do not need a branded replacement. If you want a plant-based burger every night, expect to pay for it, as you would for premium meat.

## In short

- Substitutes are often dearer, and optional.
- Savings in the Oxford study came from whole foods.
- Familiar meals can be made cheaply from staples.
