## The objection at full strength

You say the gap is closable, but I have tried. I lasted a few weeks, then something happened and I was back where I started. Why would the next attempt be any different?

## Response

A failed attempt is real information, and it is worth treating that way instead of as proof about your character. The question is what the attempt ran into. Hunger? A week of travel? A household meal you hadn't planned for? Each has a fix, and the second attempt can be designed around the first one's failure.

The [Lally et al. study](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit) of ordinary people building a daily habit is encouraging on two points. A single missed day had little effect on later gains, so one slip is not a reset. But many missed repeats did reduce how automatic the habit became, which is a reason to make the habit small enough to repeat. Time to peak automaticity averaged 66 days and ranged from 18 to a predicted 254, so some people simply need longer, and that is not a failing.

So make the target smaller than last time: one meal, one dish, a few repeats a week. For other practical snags, see [I tried and couldn't stick with it](/a/too-hard-willpower/).

## In short

- Ask what broke the last attempt and design around it.
- One slip barely matters; many missed repeats do, so keep the habit small.
- Everyone's timeline differs; longer is not worse.
