## The objection at full strength

I don't mind a vegan Tuesday dinner. But Christmas, Thanksgiving, a wedding: those are about everyone eating the same thing together. If I show up and change the menu, I'm the one spoiling the day for the family.

## Response

That is a hard moment, and the aim isn't to spoil anything. A few approaches work for many families. Offer to cook one or two dishes that everyone can share; the point of a holiday table is abundance, and a good plant-based dish is easily added. Bring your own and ask for no special treatment. Or hold the line only on what you personally eat and let others eat theirs.

It also helps to consider what the objection assumes: that a change at the table is a loss for the others. If tension does come up, it often has more to do with how the change was announced and received than with the food.

Your family may need time. Meeting them with warmth, rather than with a debate at the table, tends to earn it. Where it simply can't be done, the "as far as is possible and practicable" principle of [the Vegan Society definition](https://www.vegansociety.com/go-vegan/definition-veganism) is there for exactly that.

## In short

- Offer to bring a dish and ask for no special treatment.
- Connection comes from gathering, not one dish.
- Give family time; avoid debates at the table.
