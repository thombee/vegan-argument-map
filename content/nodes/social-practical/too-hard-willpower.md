## The objection at full strength

I did a month, then slipped. It's easy for committed people to say 'just do it', but some of us can't hold a strict rule. I cannot be the only one who has failed at this. Why set up a standard most people can't meet?

## Response

You are not the only one, and a lapse doesn't make you a failure or mean the idea is wrong. Changing a deeply set habit is hard.

What tends to help is fixing the practical causes of the slip rather than blaming willpower. Was it hunger because meals weren't filling? A missing nutrient such as B12, as the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/how-to-eat-a-balanced-diet/the-vegetarian-diet/) explains, since it is not found naturally in plant foods? A social setting? Each has a practical answer, and the [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics](https://digitalcommons.andrews.edu/pubs/648) position is that a well-planned vegan diet can meet needs at every life stage. If a diet really did leave you unwell, that is also useful information and worth taking to a dietitian.

If you would rather go in steps, that is a respectable path too, and the moral case does not vanish because the first attempt was bumpy. See [I'm cutting down; isn't that enough?](/a/reducing-is-enough/) for how I think about that.

## In short

- A lapse isn't a failure; most changes take several tries.
- Fix the cause (meals, B12, social setting) instead of blaming willpower.
- See a dietitian if the problem is health.
