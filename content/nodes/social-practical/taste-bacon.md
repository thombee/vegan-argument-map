## The objection at full strength

I'm not going to pretend there's a high-minded reason. Bacon smells great, cheese is wonderful, and a roast dinner is one of life's pleasures. Food isn't only fuel; it's joy, memory and comfort. Giving that up for a principle seems like a poor trade, and I'd rather be honest about why I eat it.

## Response

This is one of the more honest objections, and it deserves a straight answer. Taste is a real good. Nobody is asking you to pretend that food does not matter or that plants always replace animal foods exactly.

The question is what the pleasure is being weighed against. When you say "I like the taste," you are saying the enjoyment of a meal outweighs the life and treatment of the animal that provided it. Most of us would not accept that for a dog or cat: no one would say "I like the taste of dog" settles it. The difference we usually point to is that farmed animals are not companions, but they are capable of suffering, and the pleasure of a meal is not usually considered a good enough reason when the harm is something we would reject if we saw it applied to a pet.

You are not being asked to give up joy, only one source of it, and food offers many others.

That is not a verdict on you. Plenty of people eat bacon without thinking much about it, which is exactly why it is worth thinking about once.

## In short

- Taste is a real good, and it is honest to say so.
- 'I like it' is not accepted as enough when the animal is a dog.
- Enjoyment of food comes from many sources.
