## The objection at full strength

Strict vegans make it feel like you either pass or fail. Most people who hear 'all or nothing' pick nothing. If you want a kinder world, celebrate partial change and stop moralising at those who haven't gone all the way.

## Response

This is a sound point about how to talk, and plenty of vegan advocates would agree with it: meet people where they are, welcome each step, and don't make someone feel they have failed because they still eat cheese.

Welcoming steps is different from saying the steps are the goal. A doctor can congratulate a patient on smoking fewer cigarettes while still saying the aim is to stop. The two statements are compatible, and the second one is honest.

The idea of "all or nothing" is also not how veganism is defined. The [Vegan Society](https://www.vegansociety.com/go-vegan/definition-veganism) puts it as excluding exploitation and cruelty "as far as is possible and practicable", which is a standard people apply with judgment, not a pass-fail test.

So: yes to celebrating every reduction, and yes to saying plainly, and kindly, where the reasoning leads.

## In short

- Welcome every step, kindly.
- Praising progress doesn't mean the goal changes.
- Veganism is 'as far as is possible and practicable', not pass/fail.
