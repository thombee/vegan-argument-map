## The objection at full strength

If the whole world stopped using animals tomorrow, the economy would shudder: farms and abattoirs close, billions of animals are left with nowhere to go, and food supply chains face upheaval. A way of living that would be disastrous if everyone followed it can't be the right one.

## Response

Start with a fact that makes this less alarming: it won't happen overnight. Diets shift gradually as a society changes its mind, which means farms, workers and markets adjust along the way. It is a question about direction, not about a single day.

Taking the thought experiment on its own terms, modelling points the other way from disaster. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets) reports that a fully vegan diet would shrink global agricultural land from 4.1 billion to about 1 billion hectares, since around 80% of farmland currently serves meat and dairy. In a [2016 PNAS modelling study by Springmann and colleagues](https://www.oxfordmartin.ox.ac.uk/news/201603-plant-based-diets), vegan diets in 2050 cut food-related greenhouse gas emissions by 70% and avoided 8.1 million deaths a year. These are models, with assumptions, not forecasts.

The real problems are real: a fair deal for [farmers and rural communities](/a/farmers-jobs/), and what happens to the animals that already exist (see [farm animals wouldn't exist without us](/a/wouldnt-exist-otherwise/) and [animals would overrun the world](/a/overpopulation/)).

Finally, "it would be a problem if everyone did it" is a poor test of a moral view. If everyone stopped committing a wrong, the industries built on it would also be disrupted. That is a reason to manage the change, not a reason to keep the wrong.

## In short

- It would be gradual, not overnight.
- Modelling suggests far less land use and big emissions cuts.
- Disruption is a reason to plan, not to continue.
