## The objection at full strength

I live with people who eat meat and who aren't interested in this. I do much of the cooking or share a table. Making a separate meal every night is unrealistic, and I don't want a fight at every dinner.

## Response

That is a genuine constraint, and it is worth saying that the answer is not to turn dinner into a debate. You don't have to win anyone over to change what is on your own plate.

Many household meals already work in a plant-based form with the animal part added separately: a curry, chilli, pasta sauce, stir-fry or tacos built on beans, lentils or tofu, with meat offered on the side to whoever wants it. That is one pot, not two dinners. Start with the meals where you cook, since those need nobody's agreement. For more on this, see [family meals](/a/culture-family-meals/).

You can also be open without lecturing: say once, calmly, what you're doing and why, and then just do it. Accepting that others choose differently doesn't contradict the moral position, because your own consistency is the part within your control.

## In short

- Change your plate, not the household.
- One-pot meals with the meat on the side avoid cooking twice.
- State it once, calmly; skip the nightly debate.
