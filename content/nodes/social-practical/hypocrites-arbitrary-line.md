## The objection at full strength

You accept some harm and refuse other harm. Where you put the line is a matter of personal convenience. Nothing separates it from my line, which is a bit further along.

## Response

Lines that are blurry at the edges can still be clear in the middle. There's no exact moment when dusk becomes night, but nobody confuses noon with midnight. Deciding that buying a steak is on one side of the line and a road accident is on the other doesn't depend on settling every borderline case.

The line also isn't a matter of convenience in the way the objection suggests: it is drawn by asking whether the harm is deliberate and whether a reasonable alternative exists. Eating animals fails both tests for most people in wealthy countries; many other cases are less clear and vegans differ about them.

Where exactly to draw the line in hard cases is covered at [where do you draw the line?](/a/where-do-you-draw-the-line/). Uncertainty at the margins is a reason for humility in those cases, not for ignoring the clear ones.

## In short

- Blurry edges don't blur the middle.
- The line tracks deliberate versus unavoidable harm.
- Hard cases are discussed on the line-drawing page.
