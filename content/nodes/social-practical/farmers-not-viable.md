## The objection at full strength

You can't grow wheat on a hillside or in dry rangeland. A lot of farms exist because the land only supports animals. 'Switch to crops' isn't an option for them.

## Response

Some land is poorly suited to crops, and that is a real constraint. The debate over that land has its own page: [cattle graze land that can't grow crops](/a/non-arable-land/).

It doesn't account for the bulk of the picture, though. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets) reports that around 80% of the world's agricultural land is used for meat and dairy production, including cropland growing feed for animals. Land that grows feed could grow food for people directly.

For the farms that really can't grow anything else, the answer has to be about a transition of land use and income, such as restoration, other products or support payments, and that is a policy conversation. It sits alongside the ethical one, not in place of it.

## In short

- Some land is poorly suited to crops, which has its own page.
- Much farmland goes to animal feed.
- For hard cases, transition support is the question.
