## The objection at full strength

Suppose demand drops. There are billions of farm animals. They can't be released into the wild, and they can't be fed forever. The choice is between killing them anyway or letting them suffer. How is that better for them?

## Response

No serious proposal involves releasing billions of animals in a day. The practical answer is that as demand falls, fewer animals are bred. The population declines over time as existing animals live out their lives or are cared for, which is the same way any industry that shrinks gradually works.

Whether it is a loss for animals that fewer are born is a separate argument, covered at [farm animals wouldn't exist without us](/a/wouldnt-exist-otherwise/), and the worry about large numbers at [animals would overrun the world](/a/overpopulation/).

The objection treats an unmanaged crash as the only way things could go. In reality there is time to plan. The ethical point, that it is better not to breed animals into lives that end at slaughter, does not require anyone to harm animals who are already here.

## In short

- Fewer animals are bred as demand falls.
- Existing animals live out their lives; no mass release.
- The deeper arguments are on their own pages.
