## The objection at full strength

I've heard the arguments and I can't counter them. I think it's wrong. But habit, cravings and the shape of my life keep me doing it anyway. I'm not going to pretend otherwise or invent a bad excuse. At least I'm honest about it.

## Response

That honesty counts for something, and it puts you closer than most people. The hardest part of this issue for many is the first step: being willing to look at the argument and admit it holds. You've done it.

Philosophers call what you describe *akrasia*, or weakness of will: judging that one option is better and doing the other. It is a very ordinary human condition, and it is a gap, not a verdict. Two things follow. The gap is usually practical rather than a fixed fact about you. And the animals experience the action, not the belief, so the gap matters to them even while it is understandable to you.

Practical gaps respond to practical fixes. Habits are cue-driven, and the [Lally et al. habit study](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit) found new routines took 66 days on average to become automatic (with a wide range, 18 to a predicted 254 days), and that missing a single day made little difference. That is evidence that a slip is not the end. On taste, the evidence I could verify is modest: in [one experiment with toddlers](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.appet.2014.07.032), repeated tasting of an unfamiliar vegetable increased how much they ate, and the effect lasted six months. It doesn't prove adult meat cravings fade, and I won't claim it does, but it fits the general pattern that familiarity shapes what we reach for.

Concrete first steps that keep the moral position intact (animals are not ours to use) and don't demand perfection:

- Pick one meal a day and make it fully plant-based. Choose the one you already find easiest.
- Learn three go-to dinners you actually like, so the default isn't a decision.
- Plan the awkward situations in advance: what you'll order, what you'll bring.
- Get a reliable B12 source if you eat mostly plant-based, as the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/how-to-eat-a-balanced-diet/the-vegetarian-diet/) advises.

For the broader "is cutting down enough" question, see [I'm cutting down; isn't that enough?](/a/reducing-is-enough/).

## In short

- Agreeing it's wrong is the hard part; the rest is a practical gap, not a fixed trait.
- A single slip barely affects habit formation; consistency over weeks does the work.
- Start with one meal and a few go-to dishes, and keep the principle.
