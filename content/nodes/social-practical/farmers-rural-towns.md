## The objection at full strength

Whole regions are built around livestock: abattoirs, feed mills, sale yards, trucking. A small farmer can't just put in a vegetable plot and compete. Telling those people to adapt is easy from a city flat.

## Response

That is a fair point about where the advice is coming from, and a gradual, supported shift is the sensible way to handle it. The people who will bear the cost of change are not the ones telling them to make it.

Two things follow. First, the speed matters: diets change over years, and a transition that runs alongside retraining, new markets and public support is very different from a sudden collapse. Second, this is a question about how to manage a change, and it needs the same seriousness we apply to other industries that have shrunk, such as coal.

The [Frontiers study](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2023.1122992/full) of 27 farms that left livestock found that support organisations seemed particularly helpful for those moving for compassion reasons, which suggests that support helps. It also found that some moves were driven by economic interest, not conviction. That does not prove it works for every region, and it should not be oversold.

"Rural communities will be hurt" is, in the end, a reason to plan a fair transition. It is not a reason to say the underlying question doesn't matter.

## In short

- A gradual, supported transition is the responsible approach.
- Support organisations seemed to help in the study.
- Hardship is a reason to plan, not to stop asking.
