## The objection at full strength

I've tried the vegan bacon and the vegan cheese. They're not the same. If I'm giving something up, at least give me something equal. Until it tastes as good, why would I switch?

## Response

Often true: a lot of cheese substitutes in particular are plainly weaker, and anyone who says they're identical is not helping their cause.

It doesn't change the underlying question, though. A weaker alternative would only make the choice a bigger sacrifice, and a sacrifice is something people regularly accept when they think it matters. If the objection is "it's a cost to me", the answer is that the cost is real and the animals' side of the ledger is larger. If the objection is "I would switch if it tasted equal", it is worth noticing that this means the underlying ethics are not what is stopping you, and that is a useful thing to know about yourself.

Plenty of dishes (curries, pasta, dals, stews) are not imitations of anything and are good in their own right. See also [I'll wait for lab-grown meat](/a/lab-meat-later/).

## In short

- Some substitutes are weaker, and that's fine to admit.
- A cost to you is a reason to feel the weight, not to ignore the animal's side.
- Many dishes aren't imitations at all.
