## The objection at full strength

Even in your own terms, the maths favours widespread reduction. If millions of people halved their meat, the total effect would exceed what a small group of committed vegans achieves. Why not back the approach that works at scale?

## Response

Both can be true. A large population making modest changes can add up to more than a small group changing completely, and nobody should discourage it.

The two approaches support each other, though. The idea that animals are not ours to use is what gives the reduction meaning and keeps it going. Without it, people tend to reduce when it is convenient and drift back when it is not. Vegans, who have made a firm commitment, also help create the products, restaurants and norms that make reducing easier for everyone else.

And the scale of what's at stake supports aiming high. [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/land-use-diets) reports that a fully vegan diet would shrink global agricultural land from 4.1 billion to about 1 billion hectares, with around 80% of agricultural land currently used for meat and dairy. Reducing helps; the destination is further along.

## In short

- Reduction at scale and committed vegans reinforce each other.
- A firm principle keeps reductions from drifting back.
- The full change has far larger land and animal effects.
