## The objection at full strength

Adolf Hitler is widely said to have been a vegetarian and to have cared about animals. If a monster held those views, doesn't that tell you something about where this kind of thinking leads?

## Response

The facts are less clear than the claim. Goebbels wrote in his diary that Hitler was a convinced vegetarian, but the extent is disputed: [Hal Herzog](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/animals-and-us/201111/was-hitler-a-vegetarian-the-nazi-animal-protection-movement) in Psychology Today suspects he was an inconsistent vegetarian, and a colleague says he occasionally ate sausage. 

Even if he had been strictly vegetarian, the logic fails. Whether a claim is true does not depend on who else believed it. The same applies to any view a bad person happened to hold. A bad person can hold a correct view for any reason, just as good people hold mistaken ones.

Herzog makes the same point: "Simply because Hitler was (mostly) vegetarian in his dietary habits does not undermine the case for animal rights." The argument for animals stands or falls on its merits.

## In short

- His vegetarianism is disputed.
- Who held a view doesn't decide if it is true.
- The case for animals rests on its own reasons.
