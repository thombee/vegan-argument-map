## The objection at full strength

You can read dominion as gently as you like, but Genesis 9 says meat is food, and in Luke 24 the risen Jesus eats broiled fish. Dietary freedom is part of the faith. Telling a believer to avoid meat is telling them to be holier than their own scripture.

## Response

The texts are as stated. [Genesis 9:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+9%3A1-4&version=NRSV) says "Every moving thing that lives shall be food for you", and [Luke 24:42-43](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+24%3A41-43&version=NRSV) has Jesus given broiled fish, which he eats. A vegan who claims scripture forbids meat across the board would be overreaching, and this site does not claim that.

The argument is different. It is that a thing can be permitted without being what compassion asks of you today. Believers regularly make this distinction: many things are allowed and are still better left undone. The relevant question is what the text implies for a modern believer who has plentiful alternatives and knows how animals are kept and killed on large scale. Believers reach different conclusions about what that calls for.

So this doesn't have to be a contest between a vegan and the Bible. It can be an honest conversation inside a tradition about what gentleness and care for creation call for in practice.

## In short

- Permission is not obligation.
- Believers differ on what care for creation requires.
- This is a conversation within faith, not against it.
