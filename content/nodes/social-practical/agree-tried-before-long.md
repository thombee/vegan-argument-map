## The objection at full strength

If it takes more than two months before it feels normal, that's two months of low-level hassle on top of a busy life. I'm not sure I have that in me.

## Response

Fair, and nobody should promise otherwise. But "66 days" in the [habit research](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit) is the average time to reach the plateau of maximum automaticity, not the length of time something feels hard. I haven't found evidence on how the effort curve looks for diet changes specifically, so I won't give you one. What you can control is how much you attempt at once: one meal is a far lighter load than a whole diet, and you can add the next meal when the first stops costing thought.

Remember the comparison from [It's too hard](/a/too-hard/): the inconvenience falls on you for a while; the cost of not changing falls on the animals for their whole lives. That is a reason to go gradually, and a reason not to stop.

## In short

- 66 days is time to full automaticity, not time spent struggling.
- Start with a load you can carry and add to it.
- Gradual is fine; stopping is the thing to avoid.
