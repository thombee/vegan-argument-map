## The objection at full strength

I'm not against changing; I just want to do it without losing meat. Cultured meat is real meat grown without slaughter. When it is cheap and tastes the same, I'll switch happily. Until then, I'd rather wait for the technology than give up something I enjoy for a gap-filler.

## Response

Nothing in the vegan argument opposes cultivated meat in principle. If it can replace slaughter at scale, it would be a good development.

The issue is the word "wait". The animals involved are being killed now, and progress is uncertain. The [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-ingredients-packaging/human-food-made-cultured-animal-cells) lists a small number of completed consultations: its first for cultured chicken in November 2022, a second in March 2023, and one for cultured pork fat cells in March 2025. That is real progress, but it is a long way from products that are widely available and priced for everyday shoppers, and It is uncertain when, or whether, they will get there.

Waiting also has a quiet cost: people who plan to switch later tend to carry on until that day. If you are in favour of the technology, the consistent thing is to act on your concern in the meantime, and plenty of plant-based meals are available right now.

If it arrives and is widely accepted, many vegans will be happy to be proven right about the direction. In the meantime, there is little reason to put the question on hold.

## In short

- Nothing in veganism opposes cultivated meat in principle.
- Animals are being killed now; the timeline is uncertain.
- Waiting often means continuing indefinitely.
