## The objection at full strength

Fine, say the choice affects animals. But people disagree about this. Some think animals have rights, others think they're food. Different cultures and religions draw the line in different places. Nobody has a hotline to moral truth, so why should your answer carry more weight than mine?

## Response

Nobody has a hotline, and that is a reason to argue from shared ground rather than from authority. On this issue the shared ground is wide. Most people already believe it is wrong to cause animals needless suffering, and many laws are written on that assumption. Even the UK, which still permits farming, passed the [Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/22/contents), establishing an Animal Sentience Committee.

The vegan argument is mostly one of consistency: if needless suffering is wrong, and eating animal products is not needed for most people in places with plentiful food, then the position most of us already hold points one way. You can resist that by showing the premises are false or by explaining why the pig is different from the dog. That is just ordinary moral debate, and it is a better answer than "who is to say?"

Disagreement also does not make every view equal. People once disagreed about slavery, and the existence of disagreement did not show there was no better answer.

## In short

- Most people already hold the premise: needless animal suffering is wrong.
- The argument is about consistency, so test the premises.
- Disagreement does not mean every position is equally good.
