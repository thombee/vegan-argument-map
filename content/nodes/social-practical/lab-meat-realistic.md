## The objection at full strength

Most people will never give up the taste and habit of meat on moral grounds. Technology that makes meat without animals is the realistic route, so putting energy into persuading individuals is a waste.

## Response

It could be part of the answer, and pessimism about changing individual habits is not baseless. Substitutes that match the experience of eating meat could reach people that arguments don't.

But the idea of a single route is not well supported. The regulatory record is early: the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-ingredients-packaging/human-food-made-cultured-animal-cells) has completed only a handful of consultations on cultivated products. Betting everything on one technology while animals are farmed in the meantime is risky, and the path doesn't require choosing: persuasion, plant-based products and cultivated meat can all advance at once.

Also, the two aims are linked. People who have already come to see the problem are among the most likely first customers, and public concern is what creates a demand for alternatives. Ethical conviction and technology tend to move together.

## In short

- Cultivated meat may reach people arguments don't.
- The regulatory record is still early.
- Persuasion, plant-based foods and cultivated meat can all run in parallel.
